# Polymath editorial research flywheel

Status: governing production standard for Aloha AI Decision Desk, Polymath newsletters, Source Desk, System X-Rays, masterclasses, tools, briefs, and derivative content.

## Completion rule

An article is not “complete,” “source-backed,” or “Beehiiv-ready” because it contains several links. It is complete only when the editorial package contains:

1. a scoped research question and decision audience;
2. a claim ledger covering every material factual, legal, scientific, technical, historical, market, and quantitative assertion;
3. a primary-source archive or stable source record;
4. a source-to-claim map with pinpoint support;
5. a search log showing databases, sites, terms, dates, and stopping rule;
6. an authority and provenance assessment for every source;
7. contradiction, uncertainty, limitation, and missing-evidence logs;
8. current-law/current-product freshness verification;
9. a visual-evidence and photojournalism ledger;
10. a fact-check pass independent from prose drafting;
11. a citation and link-integrity pass;
12. Further Reading & Learning selected for utility, not padding;
13. derivative assets returned to the wider Aloha AI/Polymath system; and
14. a release gate recording reviewer, date, remaining limitations, and publication authorization.

If a proposition cannot be supported, it must be removed, narrowed, attributed, labeled as analysis, or explicitly identified as unknown.

## The flywheel

### 1. Frame

- State the exact question the issue will answer.
- Identify the affected people, work system, decision owner, and jurisdiction.
- Separate descriptive questions (what is happening), causal questions (why), normative questions (what should happen), and implementation questions (how).
- Write a disconfirmation question: what evidence would change RN’s proposed finding?

### 2. Discover comprehensively

- Build a source universe before drafting conclusions.
- Search official databases, statutes, regulations, agency dockets, court opinions, standards, technical documentation, peer-reviewed literature, public records, procurement records, product terms, incident reports, and first-party organizational records as applicable.
- Record exact queries, filters, date ranges, languages, jurisdictions, and sources searched.
- Use secondary sources to discover leads and disputes, not as a substitute for accessible primary evidence.
- Continue until the stopping rule is met: successive search rounds produce no new material authority, contradiction, stakeholder category, or causal mechanism.

### 3. Acquire and preserve

- Save the source or a durable reference with title, issuing body, author, date, version, URL/identifier, access date, jurisdiction, document type, and rights status.
- For dynamic pages and product documentation, record the version or capture date and the exact proposition observed.
- Preserve PDFs, orders, complaints, rules, technical reports, datasets, and source images when lawful and technically possible.
- Never treat a search-result excerpt as the evidentiary source.

### 4. Evaluate authority and provenance

Rank sources by their relationship to the proposition, not prestige alone:

1. controlling primary authority or original data/artifact;
2. official nonbinding guidance, standards, technical documentation, or first-party records;
3. peer-reviewed original research with methods and data appropriate to the claim;
4. high-quality synthesis or reporting used for context and discovery;
5. commentary used only when clearly attributed.

For each source, record scope, methods, incentives, conflicts, jurisdiction, population/system studied, limitations, and whether it actually supports the proposed language.

### 5. Build the claim ledger

Every material claim receives:

- claim ID;
- exact proposed wording;
- claim type;
- importance and risk level;
- primary source(s);
- pinpoint page, section, paragraph, table, figure, timestamp, or data field;
- support status: direct, inferential, contextual, disputed, or unsupported;
- jurisdiction and effective/current-through date;
- counterevidence;
- caveat required in prose;
- last verified date;
- owner and release disposition.

Legal allegations must be identified as allegations unless adjudicated or admitted. Agency guidance must not be described as binding law. A voluntary standard must be labeled voluntary. Product capability and terms require current verification. Numbers require denominator, period, geography, and methodology.

### 6. Triangulate and challenge

- Seek the strongest contrary source and plausible alternative explanation.
- Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence.
- Test whether a source supports the precise magnitude, population, jurisdiction, or causal language proposed.
- Identify who is missing from the record and whose incentives shape the available evidence.
- Maintain an unresolved-questions list; uncertainty is an editorial result, not a production failure.

### 7. Report visually

Photojournalism is evidentiary reporting, not decoration.

- Prefer original reporting photography, primary-document images, archival material, maps, diagrams built from verified data, screenshots of public interfaces when necessary, and photographs that reveal the real environment or system.
- Do not use generic AI imagery, decorative robots, faux documentary scenes, or generated images presented as evidence.
- Every visual needs a purpose, factual caption, descriptive alt text, creator/institution credit, original source, date/location when known, rights basis, permission status, manipulation/cropping disclosure, and claim IDs it supports.
- A caption says what the image establishes and what it does not establish.
- Sensitive information, minors, clients, patients, employees, and private spaces require heightened consent, privacy, and necessity review.
- System X-Rays must disclose source data and analytical assumptions; they cannot manufacture precision.

### 8. Draft with evidence visible

- Draft from the claim ledger, not from memory.
- Cite material propositions at or immediately after the relevant sentence or paragraph.
- Separate sourced fact, attributed interpretation, RN’s analysis, recommendation, and open question.
- Preserve necessary length. Beehiiv layout is adapted to the reporting; reporting is not shortened merely to fit the platform.
- Include Source Desk and Further Reading & Learning as substantive article components.

### 9. Verify independently

Run separate passes for:

- factual accuracy and pinpoints;
- legal status, jurisdiction, effective date, subsequent history, and allegation/adjudication language;
- scientific methods, sample, endpoints, statistical interpretation, and generalizability;
- technical version, configuration, benchmark, and vendor-claim status;
- names, dates, quotations, numbers, links, image rights, captions, and alt text;
- internal consistency between headline, dek, body, visual, tool, and RN’s finding.

### 10. Publish once; compound many times

Each completed issue should feed:

- Decision Desk archive and reusable tool;
- Source Desk and knowledge graph;
- masterclass slides and attendee resources;
- clinic prompts and intake diagnostics;
- website topic and service pages;
- System X-Rays and documentary visual library;
- LinkedIn/video scripts and outreach—with links back to the full work;
- future update alerts and change-watch calendar;
- portfolio case study and, where appropriate, client-facing research methods.

Derivative content inherits the original claim IDs, citations, rights constraints, and freshness dates. It must not detach a claim from its caveat.

## Required issue directory

Each issue should have an evidence directory containing:

- `research-question.md`
- `search-log.csv`
- `source-register.csv`
- `claim-ledger.csv`
- `contradictions-and-limitations.md`
- `visual-ledger.csv`
- `further-reading.md`
- `change-watch.md`
- `fact-check.md`
- `release-gate.md`

## Release grades

- **R0 — concept:** question and audience only.
- **R1 — scoped:** research protocol and source universe defined.
- **R2 — researched:** search log, source register, and primary materials substantially complete.
- **R3 — evidenced:** every material claim mapped; contradictions and limitations resolved or disclosed.
- **R4 — drafted:** full prose, tool, Source Desk, Further Reading, and visual plan complete.
- **R5 — visually complete:** documentary assets verified, captioned, credited, permissioned, and integrated.
- **R6 — fact-checked:** independent claim, citation, legal/scientific/technical, link, accessibility, and visual-integrity passes complete.
- **R7 — release-ready:** Beehiiv rendering checked on web, email, desktop, and mobile; paywall and subscriber routing tested; owner release approval recorded.

Nothing below R7 is publishable. Publication, scheduling, sending, and paywall activation remain separate owner-authorized actions.
