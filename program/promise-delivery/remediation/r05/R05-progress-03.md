## 1. Purpose

This unit converts “Build Your First AI Team, in a Weekend” from a four-block curriculum preview into a complete canonical open course and learner experience. The build remains a small supervised team around one bounded, reversible workflow. It does not represent agents as employees, promise universal weekend deployment, authorize autonomous external action, or certify production readiness.

## 2. Canonical curriculum delivered

- 4 weekend build-blocks
- 8 substantive lesson records and generated lesson routes
- 9 feedback-bearing assessment contracts
- 5 current authoritative source records
- 1 capstone project
- 2 weighted rubrics
- 8 outcome mappings
- 1 explicit non-issued credential record
- 1 browser-local Agent Role Contract tool link

The four blocks are:

1. See one workflow as a system.
2. Decide what AI may and may not do.
3. Design the team and its contracts.
4. Run, evaluate, and decide whether to expand.

## 3. Learner evidence package

The course requires a Workflow Contract and system map, Agent Role Contracts, Permission Matrix, Human Control record, Evidence Ledger, failure and recovery records, and a bounded go/no-go decision. Representative evaluation includes normal, ambiguous, incomplete, adversarial, and prohibited cases.

The course preserves the following controls:

- augmentation is the default authority level;
- external sending, spending, publishing, deletion, administration, and system-of-record changes remain approval-gated;
- roles are derived from bounded work, not personas;
- prompt wording is not treated as an access control;
- approval requires a named owner, evidence, rejection authority, timeout, escalation, and recovery;
- course completion does not imply production readiness.

## 4. Delivery boundaries

Open lessons and browser-local self-records are available. Enrollment, submissions, instructor review, tutor access, grading, certificates, badges, identity verification, account-synced progress, credential issuance, and production deployment are unavailable.

Learners are instructed not to place confidential, personal, privileged, regulated, or safety-critical information into the public course exercise.
