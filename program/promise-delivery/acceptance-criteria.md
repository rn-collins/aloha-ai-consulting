# Acceptance criteria

## Universal

Every promise must have an exact public statement or interface label, all known locations, visitor expectation, present reality, maturity, defect class, disposition, dependency list, priority, and observable completion test.

## Courses

A course is delivered only when its promised modules and lessons are substantive and navigable; sources and labs resolve; assessments execute with feedback; project evidence and rubric are available; status language distinguishes open materials from enrollment; progress and credential claims match the actual persistence, grading, identity, and issuance systems; and the learner journey is verified in production.

## Builds and tools

A build or tool is delivered only when it has a canonical page, accurate status and limitations, instructions, inputs and outputs, a functioning primary action, example use, data/privacy behavior, documentation, related records, last-tested version/date, error handling, and production verification. A repository link alone is not delivery.

## Templates and downloads

The named asset must exist, match its description, be readable or downloadable through an ordinary verified action, include format/version/license or use boundaries, and produce a real browser result.

## Workspace and access

Authentication, persistence, permissions, collaboration, review, audit, monitoring, and personalization may be described as available only when their relevant happy, empty, loading, error, and authorization states work in production. Otherwise the public language must explicitly identify a demo, local-only state, preview, or planned capability.

## Services and conversion

The action must reach a real contact, application, scheduling, purchase, or scoping path; explain what happens next; avoid implying unavailable procurement or instant access; and preserve appropriate professional boundaries.

## Completion evidence

Each delivered record requires an exact PR, commit, successful release gates, deployment, production URL, observed result, verification date, and verifier. Each deferred record requires accurate public wording, a named dependency, a reconsideration trigger, and no enabled control implying present access.
