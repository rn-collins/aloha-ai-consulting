Who it's for

You've identified the work you want AI to help with — intake, content, reporting, support triage — and now you need it specified properly before money goes into a build. Maybe you have a developer waiting for a brief. Maybe you want your own team to run it by hand first. Either way, you need the design nailed down.

  • You know the workflow you want to improve.
  • You want the messy parts — edge cases, review, failure handling — thought through up front.
  • You'd rather pay for a good blueprint than pay twice for a bad build.
02"Just automate it" skips the hard 20%Open

The problem it solves

The demo always looks easy. The reality is the exceptions: the client email that doesn't fit the template, the draft that needs a human eye before it goes out, the case where the AI should stop and ask instead of guessing. Skip that thinking and you ship a workflow that works on the happy path and quietly breaks on everything else.

Workflow Design front-loads that thinking. We map the full path, define exactly where AI acts and where a person decides, write the prompts and rules, and specify what happens when things go sideways — so the build inherits a design that already survived contact with reality.

What's included

Current-state mapping

We document the workflow as it runs today, including the informal steps people don't write down.

Future-state design

A step-by-step target flow showing exactly where AI assists, where humans stay in control, and why.

Prompt & rule authoring

The actual prompts, instructions, and decision rules the workflow needs — tested against real examples from your work.

Review & checkpoint design

Where a person signs off, what they check, and how errors get caught before they reach a client.

Failure & escalation paths

What the workflow does when it's unsure — stop, flag, or route to a human — so it degrades safely.

Build handoff brief

A spec any competent developer (or our Implementation team) can build from without guessing.
