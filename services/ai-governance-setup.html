Who it's for

You're responsible for client confidentiality, quality, or a regulated obligation — and AI now touches work that carries those duties. You want your people to use it, but on rails: no client data pasted into random tools, no AI-generated claims going out unchecked, no disclosure gap you can't explain later.

  • You handle sensitive information — client, patient, financial, or proprietary.
  • Your team is using AI ahead of any policy, and you know it.
  • You need something practical people will actually follow, not a binder that gathers dust.
02"Shadow AI" is the real exposureOpen

The problem it solves

Ban it and people use it anyway, out of sight. Ignore it and confidential material ends up in tools that train on it, hallucinated facts reach clients, and no one can say who checked what. The exposure isn't the technology — it's the absence of rules and the absence of a paper trail.

This engagement replaces that vacuum with a clear, usable framework: an acceptable-use policy, concrete data-handling rules, disclosure standards, and a lightweight review process. Adoption becomes deliberate and consistent instead of hidden and risky.

This is practical operational governance, not legal advice. RN Collins is a JD candidate, not a licensed attorney. Where your obligations are legally binding — regulated industries, specific compliance regimes — we'll say so plainly and recommend you run the policy past qualified counsel before you rely on it.

What's included

Current-use assessment

An honest look at how AI is already being used across your team, including the parts nobody mentions in a meeting.

Acceptable-use policy

A plain-language policy stating what's allowed, what's off-limits, and why — short enough that people read it.

Data-handling rules

Clear guidance on what information can go into which tools, and what must never leave your walls.

Disclosure standards

When and how to tell clients, and how to label AI-assisted work, so you're never caught hiding it.

Review & sign-off process

A right-sized checkpoint for who reviews AI-assisted output before it goes out, matched to the stakes.

Rollout & training brief

How to introduce the policy so the team adopts it instead of resenting it.
