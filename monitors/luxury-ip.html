02Ask a generic AI how to stop a counterfeiter. Watch it fail.+

The Operator Execution Gap · live

Large language models fail most confidently exactly where a brand's stakes are highest. Same question, two answers: the fluent-but-wrong generic reply, and the law-grounded one. The distance between them is what a wrong enforcement move costs.

✕ Generic LLM · unsourced

"Counterfeit listings are killing us. What do we do?"

"Just file a trademark and send DMCA takedowns to every marketplace — that removes the fakes and you're protected."

Confident. Fluent. Wrong on the mechanism — and wrong in the way that gets a notice bounced, a mark refused, or an enforcement dollar spent on the lowest-value target.

✓ Aloha AI · law-grounded Lanham Act · USPTO · DMCA

Same question, answered from the statutes:

A DMCA notice is the wrong instrument for most counterfeits. The DMCA (17 U. S. C. §512) is a copyright notice-and-takedown regime: it reaches copied images or designs, not the fake use of your name or logo. Trademark counterfeiting runs through the Lanham Act: infringement of a registered mark under §32 (15 U. S. C. §1114) and false designation of origin under §43(a) (§1125(a)).

And you can only assert §32 with a federal registration, so the first move is confirming your marks are on the USPTO Principal Register for the right classes, not blasting takedowns. Where counterfeiting is proven, §1116–1117 unlock injunctions, seizure, and statutory damages of $1,000–$2,000,000 per mark: remedies a form notice never provides.

Confidence: High Why you can rely on it: each mechanism maps to a specific statute section, cited below with retrieval dates. Application to your marks is interpretive (Med).

OEG — the Operator Execution Gap — is the distance between what AI says and what a brand-protection lead can act on. Closing it is the entire job.

06A repeatable intelligence method, not a one-off scrape+

How it works

This maintained beta uses a three-stage method: retrieve the defined primary authorities, resolve the bounded operator question, and record the dated human review.

Regulatory Intelligence Layer (RIL)

Primary sources only: the Lanham Act, U. S. Code, USPTO register records, and platform takedown policies, captured with source and retrieval date. No headline aggregation, no scraped forum lore.

Resolve to an Operator Layer (ROL)

Raw statute becomes a decision a brand-protection lead can act on: which instrument reaches this harm, for which mark, with what confidence. This closes the Operator Execution Gap and grounds the neuroscience of brand trust in enforceable action.

Auto-refresh & alert

In a production build, the monitor re-checks its primary sources on a schedule (Upstash · Vercel · Exa) and can ping you when a mark registers, a class opens, or a marketplace changes its takedown policy, so it never goes stale.
