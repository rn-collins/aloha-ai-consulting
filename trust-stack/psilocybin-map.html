01What being wrong in front of a funder costs+

You are across the table from a program officer, a state licensing analyst, or an investor's counsel. You make a claim about what New Mexico's law requires. They already know the answer. If your number matches theirs, the rest of your pitch gets heard. If it does not, the conversation is over.

The meeting you spent months getting, gone the instant your fact is checkable and wrong. The grant you were positioned for, reassigned to the applicant whose numbers held up. The client engagement, because an operator siting a service center cannot act on "roughly."

The errors are not cosmetic. New Mexico's binding statutory deadline is 31 December 2027, while its Department of Health is targeting an operational start a year early, and stating one as the other loses the room. Colorado's facilitator fees have since been published in tiers, so the figure you carry must match the current rule, not a stale guess. An intelligence product that does not carry those distinctions has become the error.

02What's inside+
  • The Commercial-Launch APQ Gap Model. A lens that separates what each state's law promises from what an operator can actually do at launch, scored across Authority, Posture-to-practice, and Quantification.
  • Per-state deep dives. Oregon (ORS ch. 475A; OAR 333-333), Colorado (Prop 122; C. R. S. tit. 12 art. 170 & tit. 44 art. 50; 1 CCR 213-1), and New Mexico (SB 219), with every published fee and the unpublished ones marked as such.
  • The funder-map method. How to verify every grantmaker against its own site and 990, report grant ranges only where a source states them, and reclassify developers-masquerading-as-funders as clients, with candidate names to check.
  • A Verification Ledger. Fact by fact, source by source, marked verified / partial / dropped, with retrieval dates. The part your counterparty will respect most.
03Who it's for+
  • Regulatory and legal advisers who need to be right in a room where the other side already knows the answer.
  • Operators and investors siting or funding psilocybin programs, who cannot afford to plan against a wrong date or an unpublished fee.
  • Grant-seekers and development staff who need the funder landscape as it truly is before they burn a warm introduction on a cold guess.
