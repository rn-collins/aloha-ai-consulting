01The audience you lost was erased before anyone noticed+

A removal is loud: a strike, an email, an appeal button. Reduced distribution is silent. The post stays up. The follower count holds. The dashboard looks calm. And the thousands of new people who would have found this month's content in their recommendations simply never arrive.

By the time the drop is obvious, you have often bled a full quarter of top-of-funnel growth, and the evidence you would need to appeal has gone cold. This is the most expensive event a regulated brand faces, and it is the one almost nobody is watching for.

Health, cannabis, and psychedelic content lives permanently inside the categories platforms commit, in their own policy text, to recommend less. YouTube "reduces recommendations of borderline content." TikTok publishes a named list of what it makes "ineligible for the For You feed," and content discussing drugs or other regulated substances is on it. They admit they do it. They will not confirm when they have done it to you.

02What's inside+
  • The Nervous-System-Aware framework, with each biological property mapped to a working component of the practice.
  • A taxonomy of every documented reduced-distribution mechanism, with citable policy language (quoted where the platform's wording is exact, otherwise paraphrased) from YouTube, Meta / Instagram, and TikTok.
  • The three-layer monitoring stack: native analytics, the platforms' own account-status surfaces, and third-party checkers positioned honestly as signals, not verdicts, with a verified tool-and-price table.
  • The statistical baseline method: control limits and decision rules that separate a real drop from noise, and the 45-minute monthly review you can put on a recurring calendar hold.
  • The three-tier response protocol, mapped to NIST, with appeal-ready framing, plus copy-and-use worksheets and fully cited references.
03Who it's for+
  • Content and communications teams in psychedelic health, cannabis, biopharma, and mental health who publish under regulatory constraint and cannot afford to discover a reach collapse a quarter too late.
  • Anyone whose growth depends on recommendation surfaces they do not control.
  • Teams that need a monitoring record built to the same evidentiary standard it asks them to apply to their own suppression claims.

Evidence

  • Distribution metrics
  • Content and account events
  • Platform policy changes
  • Controlled comparisons

Method

  1. Establish a baseline
  2. Track comparable outputs
  3. Log policy and account events
  4. Test alternative explanations
  5. Escalate with evidence

Assumptions

  • Sufficient historical and comparative data are available

Limitations

  • Platform systems are opaque; the method identifies evidence and likelihood, not secret causal certainty
