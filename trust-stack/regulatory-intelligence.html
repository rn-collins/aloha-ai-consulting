05The expensive failure this prevents+

A lawyer asked a general-purpose model what the law required. It answered fluently, with case names and internal quotations. The lawyer filed it. The cases did not exist.

In Mata v. Avianca (S.D.N.Y. 2023), a federal judge imposed a $5,000 Rule 11 sanction after lawyers submitted nonexistent decisions and then stood by them. In Wadsworth v. Walmart (D. Wyo. 2025), a filing cited eight nonexistent cases; the court revoked the primary drafter's pro hac vice admission and imposed monetary sanctions. The lesson does not depend on a volatile global case count: an attorney presenting a filing must verify the authorities and the propositions for which they are cited.

Every one was the same failure: a fact about what the law requires traveled forward without being checked against a primary source. The polish of the output was mistaken for its truth. A fabricated citation is a trust rupture. The moment a reader pulls one cite and it does not resolve, the credibility of your entire record collapses.

06What's inside+
  • The IDR Engine — Ingest, Digest, Regurgitate. Ingest ranks every source by legal weight before it enters. Digest verifies, structures, and scores it. Regurgitate emits only claims that survive a decision-maker's credibility check.
  • The neuroscience of why sourced facts get acted on. Appraisal theory, predictive processing, and source-credibility research, cited to their venues.
  • The authority hierarchy. Primary versus secondary, mandatory versus persuasive, mapped onto the APA rulemaking chain (5 U. S. C. § 553).
  • Two worked failures. Mata and Wadsworth, dissected with verified sanction figures and the exact point each broke.
  • A production-ready JSON record schema with a filled example, so every claim carries its pinpoint citation, retrieval date, confidence, and change-watch trigger.
  • A maintenance-protocol design for Federal Register, eCFR, and GovInfo sources, plus the IDR verification checklist: every gate a claim must clear before it leaves Layer 0. The public page documents the method; it is not a continuously connected feed.
07Who it's for+
  • Compliance and regulatory teams standing up a defensible obligation register from scratch.
  • Legal and RegTech builders who need AI-assisted research that will not get them sanctioned.
  • Founders in regulated markets (health, cannabis, psychedelics, finance, food) who make claims about what the law allows.
  • Anyone building the layers above who needs a ground-truth foundation they can trust.

Assumptions

  • Relevant authorities can be monitored and legally accessed

Limitations

  • Coverage is bounded by jurisdiction, source availability, and cadence
