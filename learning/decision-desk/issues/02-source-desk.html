What supports “Should AI record this meeting?”

This page separates legal authority, official guidance, technical risk guidance, RN’s analysis, and open questions. It is not legal advice or permission to record anyone.

Current evidence boundary: seven states have a verified recording-law layer. Zero states have a complete meeting-capture clearance. No product has been selected or tested.

Federal recording and workplace sources

  1. 18 U.S.C. § 2510 — definitions for wire, oral, and electronic communications and interception.
  2. 18 U.S.C. § 2511 — federal interception and disclosure rules, including the participant-consent provision and its limits.
  3. 18 U.S.C. § 2520 — possible civil remedies for qualifying violations.
  4. National Labor Relations Act §§ 7, 8(a)(1), and 8(d) — protected activity and bargaining issues that must be reviewed separately from recording consent.

Access, privacy, and AI risk sources

  1. U.S. Department of Justice: ADA Requirements—Effective Communication — context-sensitive duties for covered entities. It does not say an AI transcript is always enough.
  2. General Data Protection Regulation — purpose, lawful basis, transparency, rights, processor, security, impact-assessment, and transfer rules when the GDPR applies.
  3. NIST AI 600-1 — voluntary guidance on confabulation, privacy, information integrity, human review, and testing in context.

What these sources do not prove

  • They do not prove that one notice works everywhere.
  • They do not prove that recording consent also permits transcription, summarizing, training, retention, sharing, or personnel use.
  • They do not prove that an employer, school, public body, clinician, lawyer, or community group may use the same process.
  • They do not prove that a product is accurate, secure, accessible, or suitable.
  • They do not clear any live pilot.

RN’s design analysis

The five-record model—raw media, transcript, generated summary, extracted actions, and human-approved decision record—is a governance tool. It helps teams avoid treating every output as the same record. It is not a legal classification.

The recommended default is human notes only until one meeting class, all implicated authority layers, the exact product flow, meaningful participant choice, and a fictional test plan are complete.

Open evidence work

  • Forty-four state recording layers remain unverified.
  • All seven researched states still need the other authority layers.
  • Behavioral claims need direct evidence or real study.
  • Every time-sensitive source needs a final refresh before release.
  • Independent legal review, if used, must be recorded without calling it universal clearance.
