01A useful system must answer eight questions.+

The operating standard

Knowledge — What should it know?

Scope, entities, concepts, fields, relationships, exclusions, and required context.

Evidence — Why should it trust that?

Authority hierarchy, provenance, effective dates, verification status, conflict, and uncertainty.

Logic — How should decisions work?

Rules, thresholds, branching, exceptions, suppression, fallbacks, and unresolved cases.

Model — What may AI do?

Retrieval, extraction, comparison, drafting, classification, or organization within explicit boundaries.

Human — Who remains accountable?

Review, approval, escalation, licensed judgment, maintenance, incident response, and retirement.

Quality — How is performance tested?

Test cases, expected outcomes, error taxonomy, acceptance gates, monitoring, and user evidence.

Operation — How does it survive?

Ownership, permissions, documentation, training, versioning, updates, records, and funding.

Value — What changes for the user?

Cycle time, quality, access, consistency, decision readiness, risk visibility, and reusable capacity.

02The distance between what a system says and what an accountable person can safely do.+

The Operator Execution Gap

The gap increases when sources are hidden, rules are implicit, uncertainty is collapsed, the model role is unclear, review is informal, or no owner maintains the system. Aloha AI closes the gap by making each of those elements inspectable.

  • Visible evidence: source, date, status, scope, and proposition support remain attached to the output.
  • Explicit logic: rules, assumptions, exceptions, and unresolved paths can be reviewed rather than inferred from prose.
  • Bounded model role: the model performs named tasks and cannot silently assume professional authority.
  • Accountable operation: named humans approve, monitor, correct, maintain, suspend, and retire the system.

04Confidence must not outrun the evidence.+

Claims and limits

Aloha AI may claim

  • A source was retrieved and verified on a stated date.
  • A system follows the documented workflow and decision logic.
  • A test suite produced stated results under defined conditions.
  • A public tool performs its described function with stated limits.
  • A framework or curriculum was designed and documented.
  • A monitor is maintained only when a real cadence and owner exist.

Aloha AI does not claim

  • That generated text is correct because it sounds fluent.
  • That a valid citation proves the proposition attributed to it.
  • That a dated snapshot is continuously current.
  • That a diagnostic score is a validated instrument without validation evidence.
  • That a system guarantees legal, clinical, financial, security, or compliance outcomes.
  • That AI replaces licensed or accountable human judgment.

Activation rule: candidate measures, scores, classifications, or consequential automations remain inactive until the required evidence, testing, preregistration where applicable, review, and dated activation decision exist.

05Quality includes the conditions under which people can safely use the system.+

Privacy, accessibility, and security

  • Data minimization: collect only what the task requires; classify sensitivity; define access, storage, retention, deletion, and vendor behavior.
  • Accessibility: test keyboard use, focus, heading structure, contrast, readable language, mobile behavior, form errors, alternatives, and assistive-technology compatibility.
  • Security boundaries: define permissions, secrets, authentication, least privilege, logging, incident response, and separation of public demonstrations from private operations.
06A correction process is part of the product.+

Corrections and maintenance

  • Report: identify the route, claim or behavior, source or evidence, observed date, and the correction requested.
  • Review: reproduce the issue, inspect source and version history, assess consequence, and identify affected outputs.
  • Resolve: correct or qualify the material, record the change, notify affected owners when needed, and update tests or maintenance controls.

A strong system should make it easier to challenge its answer. Provenance, logic, limitations, review, and maintenance are product features rather than documentation added after launch.

How to rely on this

Evidence, method, and boundary—together.

Do not separate the conclusion from the conditions that make it trustworthy.

Evidence

  • Primary source hierarchies with provenance, effective dates, status, conflict, and uncertainty
  • Versioned source, claim, decision, revision, incident, and maintenance records
  • Evaluation cases, expected outcomes, error taxonomies, acceptance gates, and review decisions

Method

  1. Frame the decision: define the user, output, consequence, timeframe, stakeholders, exclusions, and professional review needs.
  2. Map the current work: document workflows, sources, data, tools, handoffs, bottlenecks, informal judgment, failures, and ownership.
  3. Define the source hierarchy: state which source classes control, which provide context, how conflicts are resolved, and when rechecking is required.
  4. Build the data model: create canonical fields, types, allowed values, validation, relationships, source records, status, and version history.
  5. Write the decision logic: make triggers, branches, thresholds, exceptions, fallbacks, suppressed outputs, and end states explicit.
  6. Assign mechanism by task: use deterministic rules, retrieval, generation, structured computation, and human judgment for the jobs they fit.
  7. Design the workflow: specify roles, inputs, outputs, approvals, escalation, logging, access, retention, and ownership.
  8. Prototype with bounded data: test architecture and user behavior before exposing sensitive information or consequential decisions.
  9. Evaluate and revise: measure factual, procedural, usability, adoption, and governance failures; log evidence for every material revision.
  10. Activate through gates: do not enable scoring, automation, or consequential use until evidence requirements and accountable approvals are met.
  11. Document and train: provide SOPs, owner guidance, user training, limitations, correction procedures, and maintenance instructions.
  12. Maintain or retire: recheck sources, monitor errors, control changes, preserve records, communicate updates, and retire unsupported systems.

Assumptions

  • The relevant user, decision, consequence, timeframe, and accountable authority can be defined.
  • Source authority, conflicts, and freshness requirements can be documented rather than silently inferred.
  • The organization can assign owners for review, correction, maintenance, suspension, and retirement.

Limitations

  • Methods must be adapted to jurisdiction, profession, consequence, available evidence, and organizational authority.
  • A valid citation does not by itself prove the proposition attributed to it, and fluent generated text is not evidence of correctness.
  • A dated snapshot is not continuously current; consequential systems require named maintenance ownership and rechecking triggers.
  • These methods do not replace licensed legal, clinical, financial, security, or other accountable professional judgment.
