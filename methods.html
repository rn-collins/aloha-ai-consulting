Methods, evidence, and governance

The visible interface is the end of the method, not the beginning.

Aloha AI systems begin by defining the decision, sources, data model, rules, exceptions, model roles, human responsibilities, evaluation, and maintenance. This page states the standard, including what the work can support and what it cannot responsibly claim.

The operating standard

A useful system must answer seven questions.

Knowledge

What should it know?

Scope, entities, concepts, fields, relationships, exclusions, and required context.

Evidence

Why should it trust that?

Authority hierarchy, provenance, effective dates, verification status, conflict, and uncertainty.

Logic

How should decisions work?

Rules, thresholds, branching, exceptions, suppression, fallbacks, and unresolved cases.

Model

What may AI do?

Retrieval, extraction, comparison, drafting, classification, or organization within explicit boundaries.

Human

Who remains accountable?

Review, approval, escalation, licensed judgment, maintenance, incident response, and retirement.

Quality

How is performance tested?

Test cases, expected outcomes, error taxonomy, acceptance gates, monitoring, and user evidence.

Operation

How does it survive?

Ownership, permissions, documentation, training, versioning, updates, records, and funding.

Value

What changes for the user?

Cycle time, quality, access, consistency, decision readiness, risk visibility, and reusable capacity.

End-to-end method

From problem definition to maintained system.

01

Frame the decision

Define the user, decision, output, consequence, timeframe, stakeholders, exclusions, and professional review needs.

02

Map the current work

Document workflows, sources, data, tools, handoffs, bottlenecks, informal judgment, failures, and ownership.

03

Define the source hierarchy

State which source classes control, which provide context, how conflicts are resolved, and when rechecking is required.

04

Build the data model

Create canonical fields, types, allowed values, validation, relationships, source records, status, and version history.

05

Write the decision logic

Make triggers, branches, thresholds, exceptions, fallbacks, suppressed outputs, and end states explicit.

06

Assign mechanism by task

Use deterministic rules, retrieval, generation, structured computation, and human judgment for the jobs they fit.

07

Design the workflow

Specify roles, inputs, outputs, approvals, escalation, logging, access, retention, and ownership.

08

Prototype with bounded data

Test architecture and user behavior before exposing sensitive information or consequential decisions.

09

Evaluate and revise

Measure factual, procedural, usability, adoption, and governance failures; log evidence for every material revision.

10

Activate through gates

Do not enable scoring, automation, or consequential use until evidence requirements and accountable approvals are met.

11

Document and train

Provide SOPs, owner guidance, user training, limitations, correction procedures, and maintenance instructions.

12

Maintain or retire

Recheck sources, monitor errors, control changes, preserve records, communicate updates, and retire unsupported systems.

The Operator Execution Gap

The distance between what a system says and what an accountable person can safely do.

The gap increases when sources are hidden, rules are implicit, uncertainty is collapsed, the model role is unclear, review is informal, or no owner maintains the system. Aloha AI closes the gap by making each of those elements inspectable.

Visible evidence

Source, date, status, scope, and proposition support remain attached to the output.

Explicit logic

Rules, assumptions, exceptions, and unresolved paths can be reviewed rather than inferred from prose.

Bounded model role

The model performs named tasks and cannot silently assume professional authority.

Accountable operation

Named humans approve, monitor, correct, maintain, suspend, and retire the system.

Claims and limits

Confidence must not outrun the evidence.

Aloha AI may claim

  • A source was retrieved and verified on a stated date.
  • A system follows the documented workflow and decision logic.
  • A test suite produced stated results under defined conditions.
  • A public tool performs its described function with stated limits.
  • A framework or curriculum was designed and documented.
  • A monitor is maintained only when a real cadence and owner exist.

Aloha AI does not claim

  • That generated text is correct because it sounds fluent.
  • That a valid citation proves the proposition attributed to it.
  • That a dated snapshot is continuously current.
  • That a diagnostic score is a validated instrument without validation evidence.
  • That a system guarantees legal, clinical, financial, security, or compliance outcomes.
  • That AI replaces licensed or accountable human judgment.
Activation rule: candidate measures, scores, classifications, or consequential automations remain inactive until the required evidence, testing, preregistration where applicable, review, and dated activation decision exist.

Privacy, accessibility, and security

Quality includes the conditions under which people can safely use the system.

Data minimization

Collect only what the task requires; classify sensitivity; define access, storage, retention, deletion, and vendor behavior.

Accessibility

Keyboard use, focus, heading structure, contrast, readable language, mobile behavior, form errors, alternatives, and assistive-technology compatibility.

Security boundaries

Permissions, secrets, authentication, least privilege, logging, incident response, and separation of public demonstrations from private operations.

Corrections and maintenance

A correction process is part of the product.

Report

Identify the route, claim or behavior, source or evidence, observed date, and the correction requested.

Review

Reproduce the issue, inspect source and version history, assess consequence, and identify affected outputs.

Resolve

Correct or qualify the material, record the change, notify affected owners when needed, and update tests or maintenance controls.

Submit a correction

Inspect before trust

A strong system should make it easier to challenge its answer.

That is why Aloha AI treats provenance, logic, limitations, review, and maintenance as product features rather than documentation added after launch.
