# Decision Desk monthly masterclass briefs

Status: month-specific curriculum and research briefs; not completion claims or release authorization  
Applies with: annual masterclass program contract and month-specific completion contract

## 01 — Before You Use AI for Client Intake

- Audience: small-firm owners, supervising lawyers, operations leads, legal technologists, and implementation advisers.
- Decision: whether and how AI-assisted prospective-client intake summarization belongs in one workflow.
- Learning arc: authoritative intake record → duties and data boundaries → workflow redesign → exact configuration evidence → fictional summary scoring → human review and stop rules.
- Participant output: completed Intake Summarization Evaluation Card and bounded disposition.
- Evidence burden: prospective-client duties, jurisdiction, conflict/urgency/routing, privacy/security, product configuration, automation bias, accessibility, and correction.
- Disconfirmation: insufficient exact-product evidence or unsafe data flow produces continue-human-led or reject—not a product endorsement.
- Current state: full Issue 01 system substantially drafted; launch remains NO-GO pending recorded gates.

## 02 — Should AI Record This Meeting?

- Audience: team leads, people/HR operations, counsel, compliance, knowledge managers, and meeting owners.
- Decision: whether an AI meeting bot may enter a defined meeting class, for whom, with what notice, consent, record, retention, and exit controls.
- Learning arc: classify meeting → map participants and power → identify recording/transcription authorities → trace vendor/data flow → test capture and redaction → decide meeting-class disposition.
- Participant output: Meeting Capture Permission and Record Map.
- Evidence burden: federal and jurisdiction-specific recording layers, employment/labor and sector rules, disability/language access, vendor subprocessors/training/retention, record ownership, security, accuracy, and participant objection.
- Fictional exercise: a distributed team meeting with employees, a contractor, a customer, and sensitive agenda transitions.
- Disconfirmation: an unresolved authority layer, non-optional participation, unsafe retention, or inadequate objection path blocks capture.
- Current state: partial R1 infrastructure; seven recording layers verified, zero complete jurisdiction packets. The nationwide matrix must be bounded to the fictional scenario and target audience rather than treated as universal clearance.

## 03 — Should AI Answer Your Customers?

- Audience: small-business owners, support leaders, consumer operations, product, trust/safety, and counsel.
- Decision: which support functions and message classes, if any, AI may classify, retrieve, draft, send, or act on.
- Learning arc: decompose “support automation” → assign authority by message class → authenticate and retrieve → test escalation/representations → inspect unequal failure → set send/action limits.
- Participant output: Support Automation Authority Map.
- Evidence burden: consumer protection, sector/state obligations, privacy/authentication/security, accessibility/language, vulnerable customers, complaints/incidents, model/tool permissions, logs, reversibility, and corrections.
- Fictional exercise: billing dispute, accessibility request, safety complaint, ordinary FAQ, and account-change request.
- Disconfirmation: failure to recognize material harm, complaint, fraud, accommodation, or escalation keeps that class human-led.
- Current state: R1 research question only.

## 04 — Can Your AI Find the Right Answer?

- Audience: operations, knowledge management, IT, compliance, people teams, and internal service owners.
- Decision: which internal questions a retrieval system may answer from which sources for which users.
- Learning arc: define question class → establish source authority → map permissions and stale/conflicting records → inspect retrieval/citation behavior → set abstention and correction.
- Participant output: Knowledge Answerability and Source-Authority Test.
- Evidence burden: source ownership, access control, version/freshness, provenance, conflicting authority, privacy/security, logging, employee reliance, accessibility, and correction.
- Fictional exercise: policy, benefits, security procedure, client promise, and outdated wiki conflict.
- Disconfirmation: no authoritative source, permission mismatch, unresolved conflict, or missing citation requires abstention.
- Current state: R0; activation contract required.

## 05 — Should AI Help Choose People?

- Audience: hiring, admissions, grants, procurement, creator programs, accelerators, and other opportunity owners.
- Decision: whether AI may screen, rank, or recommend a person at a specified stage of an opportunity process.
- Learning arc: define opportunity and stakes → map criteria/proxies → inspect training/evaluation evidence → test accommodations and distributional effects → create notice/contest path → constrain human reliance.
- Participant output: Human Opportunity Decision Impact Map.
- Evidence burden: applicable civil-rights/employment/consumer rules, validity, proxy discrimination, accommodations, accessibility, notice, explanation, contestability, records, audit, and vendor change.
- Fictional exercise: a fellowship shortlist with nonlinear backgrounds, disability accommodation, missing data, and proxy features.
- Disconfirmation: invalid criteria, unmeasured subgroup harm, inaccessible process, or illusory appeal blocks automated ranking.
- Current state: R0; activation contract required.

## 06 — Can You Prove That AI-Written Claim?

- Audience: founders, marketing, communications, product, legal, scientific/regulatory, and brand teams.
- Decision: whether and how AI may draft a defined class of public claim.
- Learning arc: classify claim → identify substantiation and approver → separate source fact from inference → verify endorsements/comparisons → control version and channel → preserve correction/withdrawal.
- Participant output: Public-Claim Substantiation Gate.
- Evidence burden: FTC and sector-specific substantiation, endorsements/testimonials, comparative claims, intellectual property, confidentiality, scientific uncertainty, approval records, model provenance, and version control.
- Fictional exercise: launch copy containing performance, sustainability, customer, expert, and comparative claims.
- Disconfirmation: no adequate substantiation, authority, or traceable approval means do not publish.
- Current state: R0; activation contract required.

## 07 — Can People Trust This AI Explanation?

- Audience: health, legal, financial, benefits, safety, public-service, and regulated-information teams.
- Decision: when AI may translate complex authoritative information without converting explanation into individualized professional advice.
- Learning arc: identify authoritative meaning → define audience and action consequence → test fidelity/comprehension → preserve uncertainty and boundary → design language/disability alternatives → escalate individualized questions.
- Participant output: High-Stakes Explanation Fidelity Test.
- Evidence burden: domain authority, language access, disability access, health/literacy evidence, uncertainty, omissions, advice boundary, human escalation, correction, and adverse reliance.
- Fictional exercise: a benefits/safety notice translated across reading levels and languages with ambiguous individual eligibility.
- Disconfirmation: meaning drift, omitted exception, false certainty, inaccessible output, or advice-like personalization blocks deployment.
- Current state: R0; activation contract required.

## 08 — Can You Use Someone’s AI Voice or Image?

- Audience: media, marketing, education, archives, entertainment, communications, and cultural institutions.
- Decision: when synthetic voice, likeness, or documentary-style media may represent a real or plausibly real person.
- Learning arc: identify person/identity interest → obtain scoped permission → distinguish reenactment/simulation/evidence → attach provenance → assess labor and cultural context → plan withdrawal/correction.
- Participant output: Synthetic Media Permission and Provenance Record.
- Evidence burden: publicity/privacy, copyright and performer rights, contract/consent, deceptive practices, provenance/disclosure standards, labor, cultural authority, platform rules, security, and revocation.
- Fictional exercise: a multilingual museum short using a deceased historical figure, living narrator, and synthetic restoration.
- Disconfirmation: absent authority, misleading documentary effect, unmanageable revocation, or cultural harm requires omission or a nonsynthetic alternative.
- Current state: R0; activation contract required.

## 09 — What Should Your AI Agent Be Allowed to Do?

- Audience: operations, finance, IT, security, product, compliance, and workflow owners.
- Decision: which actions an AI agent may propose, prepare, or execute in another system.
- Learning arc: enumerate action → identify principal and authority → minimize credentials → simulate failure and rollback → require approval thresholds → monitor incidents and drift.
- Participant output: Agent Action Authority and Reversibility Matrix.
- Evidence burden: authorization, identity/credentials, financial and record consequences, least privilege, confirmation, idempotency, reversibility, logging, incident response, vendor/model/tool change, and separation of duties.
- Fictional exercise: agent drafts email, updates CRM, schedules meeting, issues refund, and changes account status.
- Disconfirmation: irreversible or ambiguous authority, unsafe credential scope, missing reconciliation, or weak stop control blocks autonomous execution.
- Current state: R0; activation contract required.

## 10 — What to Check Before Buying an AI Tool

- Audience: small-organization executives, procurement, operations, IT/security, privacy, counsel, and implementation owners.
- Decision: whether evidence and contract terms support acquiring a named AI product/configuration for a bounded use.
- Learning arc: define use/data flow → test vendor claims → map subprocessors/retention/training → review security/service/change terms → plan export/exit → record accept/renegotiate/reject.
- Participant output: AI Vendor Evidence and Contract Dossier.
- Evidence burden: exact product/configuration, data ownership and use, subprocessors, security, incident notice, retention/deletion, IP, confidentiality, service levels, change rights, audit evidence, indemnity/limits, portability, termination, and regulatory allocation.
- Fictional exercise: conflicting sales deck, DPA, security page, order form, and product settings.
- Disconfirmation: material evidence gaps, unacceptable data reuse, unilateral change, weak incident/exit rights, or configuration mismatch prevents signature.
- Current state: R0; activation contract required.

## 11 — Should AI Review a Student’s Work?

- Audience: educators, schools, training programs, instructional designers, assessment owners, and accessibility/student-support teams.
- Decision: whether AI may assess, respond to, or personalize learner work at a defined instructional stage.
- Learning arc: define learning construct → separate feedback from judgment → test validity/authorship assumptions → map development and surveillance → provide accommodations/contest → constrain educator reliance.
- Participant output: Learner-Affecting AI Use Review.
- Evidence burden: educational validity, child/student privacy where applicable, disability access, bias, authorship, surveillance, developmental context, teacher responsibility, explanation/contest, records, and procurement.
- Fictional exercise: adult professional course with formative feedback, suspected AI use, multilingual learner, and accommodation request.
- Disconfirmation: invalid inference, inaccessible process, surveillance excess, or unreviewable consequence blocks learner-affecting use.
- Current state: R0; activation contract required.

## 12 — Is It Time to Stop Using This AI?

- Audience: executives, system owners, risk/compliance, operations, audit, procurement, and affected-person advocates.
- Decision: whether evidence supports continuing, constraining, pausing, rolling back, replacing, or retiring an AI system.
- Learning arc: reconstruct original authorization → compare current use/configuration → inspect performance/incidents/workarounds → identify affected-person evidence → test rollback/exit → assign remediation/redress.
- Participant output: AI Continuation and Retirement Decision Record.
- Evidence burden: baseline and current metrics, model/vendor/policy drift, incidents and near misses, complaints, unequal effects, shadow work, contractual exit, data return/deletion, replacement risk, business continuity, notice, and redress.
- Fictional exercise: a once-bounded system whose vendor, workflow, error distribution, and human workarounds have changed.
- Disconfirmation: missing monitoring, material drift, unresolved harm, nonfunctional stop path, or unsupported continued benefit requires pause or retirement.
- Current state: R0; activation contract required.

## Shared anti-duplication rule

Each month must retain its distinct decision and instrument. Shared foundations—authority, data, affected people, exact configuration, testing, accessibility, contestability, monitoring, and stop rules—may be taught consistently, but examples, evidence, exercise mechanics, and participant outputs must be issue-specific. Reusing the Issue 01 deck with changed nouns does not satisfy this program.
