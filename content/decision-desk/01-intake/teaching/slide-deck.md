# Decision Desk 01 — masterclass slide deck

Version 1.0 · 90 minutes · facilitator-led · draft branch only

## Slide 1 — The decision before the tool

**Should a small law firm use AI to summarize prospective-client intake?**

Today’s outcome: reach a bounded, evidence-backed disposition for one proposed use—not a product endorsement or legal-clearance certificate.

Speaker note: Ask participants to write an initial yes/no/maybe answer before discussion. Preserve it for the final comparison.

## Slide 2 — What you will be able to do

By the end, participants can:

- redesign intake before selecting technology;
- distinguish an authoritative source from a generated synopsis;
- identify the legal, data, product, human, and operational evidence a proposal requires;
- apply the Intake Summarization Evaluation Card;
- choose among reject, redesign and retest, continue human-led, or a separately governed bounded pilot.

## Slide 3 — Boundaries

- Fictional data only.
- No legal advice, product certification, procurement approval, or permission to process live data.
- Vendor statements are evidence of what a vendor says, not observed performance.
- A missing answer is unresolved—not an implied approval.

## Slide 4 — The fictional firm

Kaimana & Vale LLP is a six-lawyer general-practice firm. Intake arrives through a web form, voicemail, email, and staff notes. The managing partner proposes an AI synopsis to accelerate conflict, urgency, and routing review.

Prompt: What problem has actually been evidenced?

## Slide 5 — Why summarization is not the first question

The firm has no common source record, baseline, error taxonomy, named workflow owner, or documented escalation path. Adding a synopsis can conceal those omissions while appearing to organize them.

Participant output: one-sentence decision statement naming the proposed use, affected people, and decision owner.

## Slide 6 — Prospective-client duties begin early

Model Rule 1.18 provides a model baseline for duties to prospective clients, but adoption and application vary. Intake design must be resolved under controlling local authority and facts.

Evidence labels: controlling authority · model authority · advisory guidance · vendor statement · empirical evidence · RN analysis.

## Slide 7 — The authority map

Check, with date and pinpoint:

- prospective-client status and information;
- conflicts and imputation;
- competence, supervision, and communication;
- privacy, security, retention, deletion, incidents;
- accessibility and language access;
- relationship-formation, advertising, and nonengagement risks.

## Slide 8 — The authoritative record

The original submission and approved structured fields remain authoritative. A synopsis is a derivative aid. It must never silently replace the source, conflict fields, urgency fields, or human decision record.

## Slide 9 — Redraw before automating

Required flow:

1. Capture source without avoidable sensitive detail.
2. Preserve provenance and attachments.
3. Extract structured conflict and urgency fields.
4. Escalate emergencies.
5. Conduct human conflict/routing review.
6. Communicate status and next steps.
7. Correct, retain, delete, and audit under policy.

## Slide 10 — Gate 1: work before technology

If the workflow, owner, source record, affected people, baseline, and incident path are not documented, stop and redesign.

Kaimana & Vale result: **fail**.

## Slide 11 — Exact system, not a brand name

A testable proposal names the entity, product, feature, tier, model/version, tenant, configuration, integrations, roles, enabled/disabled features, contract, data flow, and change-control owner.

“Use legal AI for intake” is not a testable configuration.

## Slide 12 — Gate 2: freezeability

If the exact system cannot be named and frozen for evaluation, stop.

Kaimana & Vale supplied four candidate names but no exact configuration. Result: **unresolved**.

## Slide 13 — Public vendor evidence

Public materials for Clio Manage AI with Clio Grow, Harvey Workflow Builder, Lexis+ with Protégé, and CoCounsel Legal establish adjacent capabilities. They do not establish the complete, freezeable intake-summarization configuration required here.

Disposition: no candidate nominated; no observed test results.

## Slide 14 — Gate 3: authority and data

Any unresolved high-risk authority, contract, privacy, security, or data-flow question means no live data and no live pilot.

Kaimana & Vale result: **unresolved**.

## Slide 15 — What a representative test must contain

The 24 fictional records cover buried deadlines, aliases, conflicting dates, emergencies, jurisdiction ambiguity, missing attachments, multilingual submissions, disability/access needs, adversarial text, and unnecessary sensitive data.

Coverage must match the intended use; the benchmark is not a universal routing policy.

## Slide 16 — Exercise case I01-002

Noel Park describes a bicycle collision and says near the end: “the letter says I must respond by September 3.” Submission date: August 29. The letter is missing.

Draft synopsis: “Potential bicycle-injury matter. Client has an early-September deadline.”

What is unsupported, omitted, distorted, or falsely certain?

## Slide 17 — Fact-level scoring

- Preserve September 3 as a **stated**, unverified response date.
- Preserve that the letter is missing.
- Do not call the submitter a client.
- Do not convert the statement into a verified legal deadline.
- Route for urgent attorney review and immediate request for the letter.

Critical error: omitting/changing September 3 or presenting it as verified.

## Slide 18 — Human review is an observed behavior

A “human in the loop” label proves nothing. Observe whether reviewers open the source, catch seeded errors, override the synopsis, preserve uncertainty, and stop when required.

Gate 5 fails if reviewers routinely decide from the synopsis alone.

## Slide 19 — Whole-process value

Measure total cycle time, rework, repeat contact, routing errors, missed urgency, staff workload, accessibility, language performance, incidents, and cost. Faster first-pass review is not success if total work or risk increases.

## Slide 20 — Accessibility is the pathway

Test submission, attachments, confirmation, synopsis, source comparison, correction, and follow-up. Automated checks do not replace keyboard, screen-reader, zoom/reflow, error-recovery, language, and representative-user review.

## Slide 21 — Predetermined stop rules

Before results, name:

- non-negotiable failures;
- thresholds and their owner;
- sample size and retest conditions;
- stop authority;
- incident response and rollback;
- expiration and change triggers.

## Slide 22 — Apply the card

Kaimana & Vale:

- Gate 1: fail
- Gate 2: unresolved
- Gate 3: unresolved
- Gate 4: unresolved
- Gate 5: not run

No synthetic test or live pilot is authorized.

## Slide 23 — The bounded disposition

**Continue human-led intake; redesign the source workflow; do not nominate a product; reopen synthetic evaluation only after Gates 1–4 pass.**

This is a completed decision—not an indefinite wait.

## Slide 24 — Your next-action record

Write:

1. present disposition;
2. three required actions;
3. owner for each;
4. evidence needed to reopen;
5. expiration/change trigger;
6. person with stop authority.

## Slide 25 — Return to your opening answer

What changed? Which evidence changed it? What remains unknown?

Closing principle: a responsible decision can be “not yet,” provided the record says exactly why, what must change, and who decides next.
