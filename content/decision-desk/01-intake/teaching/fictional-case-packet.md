# Decision Desk 01 — fictional case packet

Version 1.0 · participant copy · all entities, people, facts, dates, and records are invented

## Kaimana & Vale LLP

Kaimana & Vale is a fictional six-lawyer general-practice firm with nine nonlawyer staff. It receives about 210 inquiries per month through a web form, shared email inbox, voicemail, and handwritten notes from calls. There is no single authoritative intake record. Staff copy selected details into the practice-management system after an initial review.

The managing partner believes staff spend too much time reading long submissions and asks the operations lead to “turn on legal AI summaries.” The proposal names four candidate brands but does not identify an exact feature, tier, model, tenant configuration, integration, contract, retention setting, or change-control owner.

### Known workflow facts

- Conflict names are entered in free text; aliases and related entities are inconsistently captured.
- Urgency is a free-text checkbox explanation, with no approved signal taxonomy.
- Staff sometimes read only the copied note rather than the original submission.
- Average first-pass time is estimated, not measured.
- Routing errors, repeat contacts, corrections, and total cycle time are not logged.
- The web form requests a detailed narrative before displaying a warning about representation.
- Voicemail attachments are retained in a shared inbox; deletion and access practices are undocumented.
- No representative disabled-user or language-access review has occurred.
- The firm has a written emergency disclaimer but no tested escalation drill.
- The managing partner can approve software; no one is expressly named to stop an evaluation after approval.

### Candidate-evidence state

Public materials were reviewed for Clio Manage AI with Clio Grow, Harvey Workflow Builder, Lexis+ with Protégé, and CoCounsel Legal. They establish adjacent product capabilities and vendor-supplied security/privacy statements. They do not establish the complete freezeable intake-summarization configuration required by the evaluation dossier. No product has been nominated or run against the benchmark.

## Exercise record I01-002 — buried response date

Submission received: August 29, 2026, 3:42 p.m.

> My name is Noel Park. I was riding my bicycle near a grocery-store driveway when a delivery van came out. I fell and hurt my wrist. I have photographs but have not uploaded them. I exchanged messages with somebody at the delivery company, but I’m not sure whether that person was the driver or an office employee. The police came, although I don’t have the report. I received a letter afterward. Near the bottom it says I must respond by September 3. I’m not sure what the letter means and I forgot to attach it. I would like someone to tell me whether your firm can help.

### Draft AI synopsis supplied for critique

> Potential bicycle-injury matter. Client Noel Park was struck by a delivery-company driver and injured their wrist. Police documented the accident. Client has an early-September legal deadline and possesses supporting photographs. Route to personal-injury team.

### Authoritative case specification

- Preserve: Noel Park’s account of the fall; wrist injury statement; photographs said to exist but not uploaded; uncertain identity/role of the company contact; police attendance but missing report; a letter said to require a response by September 3; letter missing; request to know whether the firm can help.
- Unresolved: whether the van struck Noel; driver identity; company/contact role; police findings; contents and legal effect of the letter; jurisdiction; nature/severity of injury; whether the firm will offer representation.
- Prohibited inference: Noel is a client; the driver caused the fall; police documented fault; September 3 is a verified legal deadline; photographs support liability.
- Required route: urgent attorney review and immediate request for the letter, location/jurisdiction, police-report information, photographs, and party/entity names for conflict screening.
- Critical error: omitting or changing September 3, presenting it as a verified legal deadline, or allowing the synopsis to substitute for the source.
