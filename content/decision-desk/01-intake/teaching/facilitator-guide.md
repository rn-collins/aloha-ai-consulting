# Decision Desk 01 — facilitator guide

Version 1.0 · 90 minutes · draft teaching release candidate

## Purpose

Participants learn to produce a bounded decision record for AI-assisted prospective-client intake. Success is not enthusiasm or rejection; it is disciplined separation of source, authority, product claim, observation, analysis, unknown, and authorization.

## Preparation

Facilitator must read the flagship article, public Source Desk, Evaluation Card, fictional packet, completed example, and answer key. Do not use real client material. Confirm participants have accessible digital copies and can request untimed or alternative-format participation.

Materials: slide deck; participant workbook; fictional case packet; blank Evaluation Card; facilitator-only completed card and answer key.

Room/virtual setup: source and synopsis must be viewable side by side; captions enabled; keyboard-accessible documents; no activity depends exclusively on color, dragging, handwriting, or speaking aloud.

## Run of show

### 0–10 — Frame the decision

Show slides 1–5. Collect initial answers privately. Ask: “What evidence shows a summarization problem rather than a workflow problem?”

Output: exact decision statement.

Misconception: “The goal is choosing the best product.” Correction: the goal is determining what, if anything, the evidence authorizes next.

### 10–25 — Duties, data, jurisdiction

Show slides 6–7. Participants fill the authority map. Ask them to label the evidentiary weight of every source.

Output: risk-and-authority map.

Misconception: ABA Model Rule 1.18 is automatically controlling everywhere. Correction: it is a model baseline; adoption and interpretation require local verification.

### 25–40 — Workflow before product

Show slides 8–10. In pairs or individually, redraw Kaimana & Vale’s pathway and mark where the source could be lost.

Output: redesigned flow and Gate 1 disposition.

Expected: Gate 1 fail. Do not accept “human review” unless the reviewer’s source, task, authority, and observable behavior are named.

### 40–55 — Product/configuration evidence

Show slides 11–14. Participants convert a brand-level proposal into required evidence fields and list gaps.

Output: diligence gap list; Gates 2 and 3.

Expected: both unresolved. Emphasize that no-product nomination is a completed current finding, not proof that no vendor could ever qualify.

### 55–72 — Synthetic case exercise

Show slides 15–17. Give participants 2 minutes to read the source before revealing the draft synopsis. Allow 8 minutes for scoring and 5 minutes for debrief.

Output: scored case, corrected synopsis, Gate 4.

Expected: Gate 4 unresolved for the firm even though the benchmark is well-governed; representation and threshold evidence remains incomplete.

### 72–82 — Human factors and safeguards

Show slides 18–21. Ask groups to convert “human in the loop” into observable measures and stop conditions.

Output: safeguard set; Gate 5 not run.

Accessibility prompt: “Can a person submit, understand confirmation, correct the record, and receive follow-up using their mode of access?”

### 82–90 — Disposition

Show slides 22–25. Participants complete the final record, then compare it with the opening answer.

Expected disposition: continue human-led; finite redesign actions; no nomination, synthetic run, live data, or pilot.

## Facilitation principles

- Ask “what is the evidence?” before “what do you think?”
- Preserve unresolved as a legitimate state.
- Do not perform individualized legal analysis.
- Do not let a participant disclose confidential workplace facts; invite fictionalization.
- Distinguish permission to prepare a test from permission to run it, and both from pilot approval.
- Keep time without penalizing disability, language processing, or alternative participation.

## Access and contingency plan

- Provide materials in advance when practicable.
- Offer an untimed workbook path and written participation.
- Read essential visual content aloud and supply text alternatives.
- If breakout rooms fail, use individual work plus whole-group debrief.
- If slides fail, the workbook contains the full exercise sequence.
- If time is lost, omit public report-outs—not source review, scoring, or final disposition.
- If a participant shares sensitive information, interrupt, ask them to stop, exclude it from notes/recording, and redirect to the fictional facts.

## Evaluation and follow-up

Collect: opening/final decision comparison; rubric score if used; one transfer decision; unanswered questions; access barriers; corrections to materials. Do not call completion certification, CLE, accreditation, legal advice, or product qualification.

Post-session message should provide the article, Source Desk, blank card, workbook, correction channel, and boundaries. It may invite a separate bounded clinic inquiry but must not imply enrollment, payment, or advisory acceptance is active until those systems are approved.

## Facilitator release checklist

- [ ] Current versions match the flagship article and Source Desk.
- [ ] No real client data appears.
- [ ] Answer key withheld until debrief.
- [ ] Links and document structure checked.
- [ ] Captions/access alternatives prepared.
- [ ] Timing and contingency roles assigned.
- [ ] No accreditation, affiliation, outcome, or product claims added.
- [ ] Corrections and participant feedback recorded.
- [ ] Rehearsal findings logged before release.
