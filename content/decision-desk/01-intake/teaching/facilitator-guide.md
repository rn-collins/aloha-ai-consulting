# Decision Desk 01 — facilitator guide

Version 1.1 · 60 minutes · draft teaching release candidate

## Purpose

Participants learn to produce a bounded decision record for AI-assisted prospective-client intake. Success is not enthusiasm or rejection; it is disciplined separation of source, authority, product claim, observation, analysis, unknown, and authorization.

## Preparation

Facilitator must read the flagship article, public Source Desk, Evaluation Card, fictional packet, completed example, and answer key. Do not use real client material. Confirm participants have accessible digital copies and can request untimed or alternative-format participation.

Materials: slide deck; participant workbook; fictional case packet; blank Evaluation Card; facilitator-only completed card and answer key.

Room/virtual setup: source and synopsis must be viewable side by side; captions enabled; keyboard-accessible documents; no activity depends exclusively on color, dragging, handwriting, or speaking aloud.

## Run of show

### 0–7 — Private opening view
Participants write the decision and one assumption before discussion. Output: bounded statement.

### 7–16 — Authority, people, and data
Participants label source weight and affected people. Do not let seniority set the answer. Output: authority map.

### 16–28 — Actual work
Individually, then together where appropriate, map sources, handoffs, workarounds, exceptions, judgment, access needs, and downstream reliance. Output: actual-work map and Gate 1.

### 28–37 — Evidence and disconfirmation
Convert brand claims into evidence fields; ask what would make the proposal wrong. Output: gaps and Gates 2–3.

### 37–49 — Synthetic case
Score independently before debrief. Invite the strongest alternative and record disagreement. Output: corrected synopsis and Gate 4.

### 49–57 — Implementation and maintenance
Turn “human in the loop” into observable behavior, if–then action, stop authority, escalation, and review trigger. Output: safeguard/maintenance set.

### 57–60 — Disposition
Record disposition, owner, next evidence, change trigger, dissent, and what is not authorized. Compare with opening view.

## Facilitation principles

- Apply the canonical participatory decision and implementation standard.
- Collect individual input before discussion; preserve dissent and unknowns.
- Ask what actual practice, another role, or disconfirming evidence could change the map.
- Ask “what is the evidence?” before “what do you think?”
- Preserve unresolved as a legitimate state.
- Do not perform individualized legal analysis.
- Do not let a participant disclose confidential workplace facts; invite fictionalization.
- Distinguish permission to prepare a test from permission to run it, and both from pilot approval.
- Keep time without penalizing disability, language processing, or alternative participation.

## Access and contingency plan

- Provide materials in advance when practicable.
- Offer an untimed workbook path and written participation.
- Read essential visual content aloud and supply text alternatives.
- If breakout rooms fail, use individual work plus whole-group debrief.
- If slides fail, the workbook contains the full exercise sequence.
- If time is lost, omit public report-outs—not source review, scoring, or final disposition.
- If a participant shares sensitive information, interrupt, ask them to stop, exclude it from notes/recording, and redirect to the fictional facts.

## Evaluation and follow-up

Collect: opening/final decision comparison; rubric score if used; one transfer decision; unanswered questions; access barriers; corrections to materials. Do not call completion certification, CLE, accreditation, legal advice, or product qualification.

Post-session message should provide the article, Source Desk, blank card, workbook, correction channel, and boundaries. It may invite a separate bounded clinic inquiry but must not imply enrollment, payment, or advisory acceptance is active until those systems are approved.
