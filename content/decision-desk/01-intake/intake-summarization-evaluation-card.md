## Source and change record

For every authority, study, vendor document, and internal dataset, record title, issuer, date, version, URL/file location, pinpoint, authority level, limitation, and last verification date.

New Mexico requires separate treatment of controlling and advisory authority. Rule 16-118 NMRA governs prospective-client information and conflicts. Formal Ethics Advisory Opinion 2024-004 permits responsible generative-AI use only within existing duties and emphasizes confidentiality, vendor practices, supervision, and independent verification. It does not authorize AI intake summarization as a category or establish that a named tool is accurate, secure, or effective; those questions remain product- and configuration-specific gates.

New York also requires authority-level separation. Statewide Rule 1.18 governs prospective-client information, conflicts, and the circumstances in which invited electronic submissions become consultations, including the effect of clear warnings and the need to limit initial exposure. NYC Bar Opinions 2024-5 and 2025-6 provide persuasive local guidance on generative AI and AI-assisted conversation summaries but are not statewide controlling law. Neither the rule nor those opinions authorizes AI intake summarization as a category or establishes that a named tool, version, or configuration is accurate, secure, accessible, or effective.

North Carolina requires three distinct authority layers. Rule 1.18 controls prospective-client information and conflicts and expressly addresses invited electronic submissions, clear warnings, limited initial collection, screening, and notice. Adopted 2011 Formal Ethics Opinion 8 applies those duties to human-operated live-chat intake, while adopted 2024 Formal Ethics Opinion 1 governs generative-AI use generally. Neither opinion authorizes AI intake summarization as a category or establishes that a named tool, model version, vendor arrangement, or configuration is accurate, secure, accessible, or effective.

North Dakota Rule 1.18 controls prospective-client information, conflicts, invited electronic submissions, clear warnings, limited initial collection, consent, exposure controls, and notice. No adopted North Dakota AI-specific ethics guidance was located in the bounded official-source search, so that field remains open rather than being converted into an absence conclusion. The rule does not authorize AI intake summarization or establish that a named tool, model version, vendor arrangement, or configuration is accurate, secure, accessible, or effective.

Ohio requires two distinct authority layers. Rule 1.18 controls prospective-client information, conflicts, invited electronic submissions, clear warnings, limited initial collection, consent, screening, and notice. The 2026 Ohio Ethics Guide is expressly nonbinding Board-staff guidance; it requires careful vendor and data-practice review and independent verification of AI-generated summaries against original source materials. Neither source authorizes AI intake summarization as a category or establishes that a named tool, model version, vendor arrangement, or configuration is accurate, secure, accessible, or effective.

Oklahoma Rule 1.18 controls prospective-client information, conflicts, invited written, oral, and electronic submissions, clear warnings, limited initial collection, consent, exposure controls, screening, and notice. No Oklahoma-adopted AI-specific ethics opinion was located in the bounded official-source search, so that field remains open rather than being converted into an absence conclusion. The rule does not authorize AI intake summarization or establish that a named tool, model version, vendor arrangement, or configuration is accurate, secure, accessible, or effective.

Oregon Rule 1.18 controls prospective-client information, conflicts, consent, reasonable limits on exposure to disqualifying information, screening, and notice. Formal Opinion 2025-205 governs AI tools generally, and Formal Opinion 2026-208 directly addresses chatbots and AI agents used with prospective clients. The chatbot opinion requires competence, security and data-handling review, vendor diligence, monitoring, supervision, relationship-formation controls, nonmisleading communications, safeguards against legal advice and improper solicitation, and confidentiality protection. These authorities support product-specific gates; they do not establish that a named intake-summarization product, model, disclaimer, monitoring workflow, or human-review process is accurate, secure, accessible, or effective.

Pennsylvania Rule 1.18 controls prospective-client status, electronic communications, confidentiality, conflicts, consent, limited initial collection, screening, notice, and advance consultation conditions. Joint Formal Opinion 2024-200 separately addresses lawyers' AI competence, confidentiality, conflicts, verification, supervision, communication, judgment, billing, and transparency at expressly advisory and nonbinding weight. These sources require careful intake design and product-specific evaluation; they do not authorize AI intake summarization as a category or establish that a named tool, model version, vendor arrangement, warning, or human-review process is accurate, secure, accessible, or effective.
