# Contradictions, limitations, and unresolved questions

Status: active R2 research record; not a legal conclusion or publication-ready article.

## Scope limitation

There is no single controlling nationwide professional-responsibility answer for a “small U.S. law firm.” ABA Model Rules and ABA formal opinions are model or persuasive authorities, while controlling rules, ethics opinions, statutes, court orders, privacy duties, recording laws, and records requirements vary by jurisdiction. The final tool must include a jurisdiction field and a local-authority verification gate.

## Authority distinctions that must remain visible

- ABA Formal Opinion 512 is important national guidance, but it is not binding nationwide.
- NIST AI 600-1 is a voluntary cross-sector profile, not a rule of professional conduct.
- The FTC Office of Technology post is an official regulator statement, not a regulation or adjudicated finding.
- Florida Opinion 24-1 expressly identifies itself as advisory and nonbinding.
- Florida Opinion 24-1 is nevertheless intake-specific in part: its advertising section addresses generative-AI chatbots used for client intake, disclosure, misleading communications, and screening. It must not be described as "not intake-specific."
- New York City Bar opinions must not be described as controlling every New York lawyer.
- California's 2026 Practical Guidance replaces its 2023 guidance; stale references to the 2023 version must be removed or historically labeled.
- Virginia LEO 1901 moved from proposal to approval effective November 24, 2025; proposal-stage descriptions are stale.
- Michigan RI-392 concerns access to and disposition of client files after representation. It is not an artificial-intelligence opinion and cannot support an AI proposition. Michigan AI and intake analysis must instead rely on the current court-adopted rules and separately identified AI materials, with their authority levels kept distinct.
- Michigan's AI FAQs are official State Bar guidance, but not a formal ethics opinion. They supply general AI-practice cautions rather than prospective-client intake validation.
- Minnesota Rule 1.18 and its comments address invited electronic submissions, warnings, and limited initial collection. The lawyer-discipline office article is educational; neither source establishes the accuracy or safety of an AI intake system.
- Missouri Informal Opinion 2024-11 is general, nonbinding AI-practice guidance. It must not be collapsed into Rule 4-1.18 or described as intake-specific approval.
- Nebraska Supreme Court Rule § 3-501.18 supplies the controlling prospective-client baseline, including unilateral-communication and informed-consent boundaries. It does not address or validate AI intake summarization, and no intake-specific Nebraska AI authority is claimed.
- Montana's controlling prospective-client provision is Rule 1.20, not Rule 1.18; Montana Rule 1.18 governs IOLTA. Rule 1.20 verifies the consultation, confidentiality, conflict, limited-exposure, consent, screening, and notice baseline, but it does not address or validate AI intake summarization. Mississippi remains open because no current issuing-body prospective-client text with a reliable pinpoint was acquired in this pass; that is an acquisition status, not an absence finding.
- Arizona's 2024 Supreme Court committee guidance expressly discusses disclosure when prospective clients communicate with AI, but it is nonbinding guidance and cannot substitute for the controlling Arizona rules or product-specific evidence.
- Kentucky E-457 is formally adopted bar guidance on lawyers' use of AI generally. It expressly leaves advertising questions outside its scope and does not validate prospective-client intake summarization.
- Idaho's current consolidated court-adopted Rule 1.18 is now verified. Its adopted comments address invited electronic submissions, clear warnings, and limiting initial collection, but the rule does not validate AI summarization or establish that every unilateral message is a consultation.
- Arkansas's current professional-conduct index and a March 2026 disciplinary decision verify the Rule 1.18(b) prospective-client confidentiality baseline. Arkansas Supreme Court Administrative Order 25 is an adopted court-administration order concerning confidential or sealed case information disclosed to AI tools; it is not a private-law-firm intake rule or product validation.
- Alabama Formal Opinion 2026-01 is current formal AI guidance, but it is general rather than prospective-client-intake-specific. The State Bar's Client Keeper handbook recommends limited initial collection and conflict screening, but it is risk-management guidance. No Alabama Rule 1.18 analogue is claimed until controlling authority is acquired.
- Georgia's current rules index verifies adopted Rule 1.18. The State Bar's July 2026 Generative AI Toolkit expressly addresses chatbots and virtual assistants used for prospective-client intake, but it is advisory practice guidance rather than a formal opinion, court rule, or product validation.
- Louisiana's Supreme Court adoption order verifies Rule 1.18(a)-(b) as the controlling prospective-client baseline. It does not supply intake-specific AI guidance or product validation, and later-amendment monitoring remains open.
- Maine's current court compilation verifies Rule 1.18 and Comment 4's instruction to limit an initial interview to information reasonably necessary for the representation decision. That intake-design baseline does not validate AI summarization.
- Maryland's Rule 19-301.18 and Comment 4 supply the controlling prospective-client baseline. June 2026 language that would add artificial intelligence tools to Rule 19-301.6 Comment 20 appears in a rules-committee agenda and must remain proposal-stage unless and until a final Supreme Court order is acquired.
- The jurisdiction matrix is an acquisition-status record, not a 50-state legal conclusion. `Open` means the controlling text has not yet been acquired and pinpointed in this package; it does not mean the jurisdiction lacks a rule or AI authority. `Partial` means an official guidance source is preserved while the controlling intake baseline or another material authority remains open.
- The pinpoint register includes only mappings already supported by the source and claim ledgers. A missing jurisdiction or claim mapping is an unresolved research item, never an absence finding.

## Disconfirming considerations

A bounded pilot should not be recommended when the firm cannot establish all of the following:

1. a controlling-jurisdiction review;
2. an approved data environment with verified retention, training, access, deletion, subprocessor, and incident terms;
3. preservation of the original submission as the authoritative record;
4. independent structured collection of party names, dates, jurisdiction, urgency, and contact data;
5. a trained reviewer who compares source and synopsis;
6. a tested error taxonomy and evaluation set;
7. stop authority and an incident path;
8. accurate nonengagement and automation communications; and
9. evidence that the pilot improves the whole intake process rather than only reducing first-pass time.

If any one is absent, the defensible finding may be “redesign first” or “do not pilot,” not a conditional yes.

## Unresolved research

- Full 50-state/current-territory inventory of adopted prospective-client rules and AI guidance.
- Controlling-law selection for a representative launch jurisdiction or expressly non-jurisdictional framing.
- Original empirical evidence on summarization fidelity in legally meaningful intake conditions.
- Human-factors evidence on automation bias, source neglect, anchoring, and reviewer behavior in comparable workflows.
- Accessibility and language-performance evidence for the intended intake population.
- Actual vendor and model configuration; data lifecycle and contract review cannot be completed in the abstract.
- Privilege, waiver, breach-notification, data-retention, recording, and consumer-privacy implications by jurisdiction and system design.
- Firm intake evidence: baseline errors, rework, cycle time, matter classes, urgency signals, and reviewer capacity.
- Exact quantitative thresholds for the pilot. The present “any invented fact” and “any missed urgency signal” rules are normative safety choices and must be labeled as such, not presented as empirically derived universal thresholds.
- Rights-cleared original reporting photography. Official-document crops require rights and fair-use/editorial review; ABA reproduction terms must be checked before embedding opinion pages.

## Empirical evidence boundary

The completed first empirical pass located no primary study directly validating generative-AI summarization for prospective-client law-firm intake. Adjacent studies cannot be converted into an intake accuracy rate:

- Nguyen et al. evaluate summarization on patent abstracts and claims, not narratives submitted by prospective clients. Their error taxonomy supports measuring incompleteness separately from hallucination; it does not establish expected intake performance.
- Akter et al. describe a source-linked prototype for nine public civil-deposition transcripts. It is a design demonstration, not a controlled user-outcome or operational-effectiveness trial, and it uses LLM-based evaluators in parts of the pipeline.
- NIST AI 800-4 reports that post-deployment monitoring practice and terminology remain nascent and separates human-factors monitoring from functionality, security, compliance, and operational monitoring.

The absence finding is bounded by the databases, queries, and date in the search log. Proprietary or unpublished evaluations may exist. The article must say "no directly applicable primary study was located," not "none exists."

## Revised decision sequence

The draft previously collapsed two different stages: pre-deployment evaluation and a live pilot. They must be separated.

1. Redesign and document the non-AI intake workflow.
2. Select a named product and exact configuration.
3. Complete jurisdiction, contract, privacy, security, accessibility, and data-flow review.
4. Run a pre-deployment shadow evaluation on representative fictional, synthetic, or appropriately governed historical data.
5. Compare source and synopsis using a fact-level error taxonomy, not only an aggregate quality score.
6. Evaluate reviewer behavior and total-process effects.
7. Authorize a live bounded pilot only if every predetermined gate passes.

A failure at any stage results in redesign, product rejection, or continued human-led intake.

## Required article correction

The current article must not recommend a live pilot. The defensible interim finding is “redesign and evaluate before any live pilot.” A live pilot becomes a possible later decision only after the named product and configuration pass all pre-deployment gates.
