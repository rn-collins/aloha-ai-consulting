# Contradictions, limitations, and unresolved questions

Status: active R2 research record; not a legal conclusion or publication-ready article.

## Scope limitation

There is no single controlling nationwide professional-responsibility answer for a “small U.S. law firm.” ABA Model Rules and ABA formal opinions are model or persuasive authorities, while controlling rules, ethics opinions, statutes, court orders, privacy duties, recording laws, and records requirements vary by jurisdiction. The final tool must include a jurisdiction field and a local-authority verification gate.

## Authority distinctions that must remain visible

- ABA Formal Opinion 512 is important national guidance, but it is not binding nationwide.
- NIST AI 600-1 is a voluntary cross-sector profile, not a rule of professional conduct.
- The FTC Office of Technology post is an official regulator statement, not a regulation or adjudicated finding.
- Florida Opinion 24-1 expressly identifies itself as advisory and nonbinding.
- New York City Bar opinions must not be described as controlling every New York lawyer.
- California's 2026 Practical Guidance replaces its 2023 guidance; stale references to the 2023 version must be removed or historically labeled.
- Virginia LEO 1901 moved from proposal to approval effective November 24, 2025; proposal-stage descriptions are stale.

## Disconfirming considerations

A bounded pilot should not be recommended when the firm cannot establish all of the following:

1. a controlling-jurisdiction review;
2. an approved data environment with verified retention, training, access, deletion, subprocessor, and incident terms;
3. preservation of the original submission as the authoritative record;
4. independent structured collection of party names, dates, jurisdiction, urgency, and contact data;
5. a trained reviewer who compares source and synopsis;
6. a tested error taxonomy and evaluation set;
7. stop authority and an incident path;
8. accurate nonengagement and automation communications; and
9. evidence that the pilot improves the whole intake process rather than only reducing first-pass time.

If any one is absent, the defensible finding may be “redesign first” or “do not pilot,” not a conditional yes.

## Unresolved research

- Full 50-state/current-territory inventory of adopted prospective-client rules and AI guidance.
- Controlling-law selection for a representative launch jurisdiction or expressly non-jurisdictional framing.
- Original empirical evidence on summarization fidelity in legally meaningful intake conditions.
- Human-factors evidence on automation bias, source neglect, anchoring, and reviewer behavior in comparable workflows.
- Accessibility and language-performance evidence for the intended intake population.
- Actual vendor and model configuration; data lifecycle and contract review cannot be completed in the abstract.
- Privilege, waiver, breach-notification, data-retention, recording, and consumer-privacy implications by jurisdiction and system design.
- Firm intake evidence: baseline errors, rework, cycle time, matter classes, urgency signals, and reviewer capacity.
- Exact quantitative thresholds for the pilot. The present “any invented fact” and “any missed urgency signal” rules are normative safety choices and must be labeled as such, not presented as empirically derived universal thresholds.
- Rights-cleared original reporting photography. Official-document crops require rights and fair-use/editorial review; ABA reproduction terms must be checked before embedding opinion pages.

## Required article correction

The current R1 sentence stating that the firm should “pilot a synopsis” is ahead of the evidence. Until the unresolved modules are completed, the finding must be framed as a hypothesis under investigation, not the final Decision Desk determination.
