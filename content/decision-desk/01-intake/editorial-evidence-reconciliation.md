Status: production control record; release candidate disposition; not publication authorization

Governing question: Should a small law firm use AI to summarize prospective-client intake?

## Editorial answer

**Not from the evidence presently available. Continue human-led intake, redesign the source workflow, and require a separately governed evaluation before any bounded pilot.**

This is not a finding that generative AI can never assist intake. It is a finding that the reviewed public record does not establish a named, freezeable intake-summarization product and configuration that can be responsibly evaluated or recommended for the governed use. Public vendor materials show adjacent capabilities, not the complete product, contract, configuration, data-flow, retention, audit, accessibility, and change-control evidence required by the frozen dossier.

The flagship therefore does not wait for vendor participation. Its product disposition is `human_led_only`. A later candidate may reopen product evaluation only by satisfying the recorded reopening packet; that later work is an expansion decision, not a prerequisite to the first article or masterclass.

## Claim-class disposition

The release candidate may state:

1. A prospective-client intake creates duties and risks before engagement, but the controlling rule and its application vary by jurisdiction.
2. The original submission—not an AI synopsis—must remain the authoritative record.
3. Structured collection is preferable for conflict, urgency, routing, identity, date, and jurisdiction fields that should not depend on generated prose.
4. A reviewer must compare source and synopsis; a nominal “human in the loop” is not evidence that errors will be detected.
5. Accessibility must be evaluated across submission, confirmation, source review, correction, and follow-up; automated checking alone is insufficient.
6. No reviewed candidate's public record establishes the exact intake-summarization feature and freezeable configuration required for nomination.
7. The current bounded disposition is `human_led_only`, with redesign and product-specific diligence before any future synthetic evaluation or pilot.

The release candidate must not state:

- that the ABA Model Rules or ABA Formal Opinion 512 are binding nationwide;
- that every website submission creates identical prospective-client duties;
- that any ethics authority validates a named product or configuration;
- that no suitable product exists anywhere;
- that adjacent summarization or clinical automation-bias studies establish a law-firm error rate;
- that the 51-row matrix supplies nationwide legal clearance;
- that the frozen 24-record benchmark contains observed product results;
- that accessibility is established by automated testing or technical conformance alone;
- that a vendor's public statement is independent validation or a negotiated contractual commitment.

## Source Desk architecture

The public article's present 50-item reading list is not the canonical Source Desk and must not be represented as one. It is a curated public bibliography. The release-ready Source Desk must be generated from the canonical registers and must provide, for each material public claim:

- claim ID and release wording;
- source ID, issuing body, authority level, and jurisdiction;
- direct link and usable pinpoint;
- support relation;
- material limitation or counterevidence;
- current-through or last-verified date; and
- disposition.

The public Source Desk may omit duplicative acquisition records, sealed benchmark answers, and nonmaterial background sources, but every omission must leave the material claim auditable. Public display IDs must map deterministically to canonical IDs rather than silently renumbering sources.
