# Research question

Under what factual, professional-responsibility, privacy, security, records, vendor, and human-factors conditions—if any—should a small U.S. law firm use generative AI to summarize information submitted by a prospective client?

## Decision audience

Small-firm managing partner, intake lead, ethics counsel, privacy/security owner, and the person accountable for vendor approval.

## Disconfirmation question

What evidence would require a finding of “do not pilot,” rather than a bounded assistive pilot?

## Required research modules

1. Prospective-client duties and formation/nonformation communications.
2. Confidentiality, competence, communication, supervision, conflicts, records, and fees.
3. Jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction adoption and AI ethics guidance for the launch audience.
4. Vendor data lifecycle: input, logs, retention, training, subprocessors, geography, deletion, incident response, privilege/confidentiality positions.
5. Failure modes: omission, fabrication, attribution, deadline/urgency loss, anchoring, automation bias, disparate performance, and source neglect.
6. Intake workflow evidence and human-factors research.
7. Evaluation design, thresholds, stop authority, incident response, and auditability.
8. Client/prospective-client notice, consent, accessibility, language, and expectations.
