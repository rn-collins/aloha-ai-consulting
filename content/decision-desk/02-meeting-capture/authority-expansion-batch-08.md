## New Hampshire — verified all-parties framework

### Controlling current text

1. New Hampshire General Court, RSA 570-A:2, Interception and Disclosure of Telecommunication or Oral Communications Prohibited:  
   https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LVIII/570-A/570-A-2.htm
2. New Hampshire General Court, RSA 570-A:11, Recovery of Civil Damages Authorized:  
   https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LVIII/570-A/570-A-11.htm
3. New Hampshire General Court, consolidated Chapter 570-A:  
   https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/lviii/570-a/570-a-mrg.htm

Issuer: New Hampshire General Court. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- RSA 570-A:2, I makes willful interception, specified device-assisted interception, and knowing use or disclosure unlawful unless the chapter specifically provides otherwise or all parties consent.
- Paragraph I-a separately makes it a misdemeanor to knowingly intercept when the person is a party or has one party’s prior consent, but lacks all parties’ consent and the approval required by paragraph II(d).
- Paragraph II(d) is a bounded investigative or law-enforcement route requiring specified official approval and reasonable suspicion; it is not a private workplace, classroom, advisory, or meeting-bot exception.
- Other paragraph II exceptions are role- and context-specific, including carriers, emergency services, public utilities, law enforcement, and school buses; they do not create general permission for ordinary organizational meetings.
- RSA 570-A:6 restricts evidentiary use when disclosure would violate the chapter.
- RSA 570-A:11 supplies a civil action for qualifying interception, disclosure, or use and identifies actual or statutory damages, punitive damages, reasonable attorney’s fees, and litigation costs under its terms.
- Consent to interception does not by itself establish specific, informed, accessible, voluntary permission for transcription, summarization, model processing, retention, disclosure, or publication.

### Bounded claim

For an ordinary private meeting in New Hampshire, documented consent from every party is the conservative rule supported by the current statutory text. A participant’s presence or one party’s permission alone does not supply a general private-person exception under RSA 570-A:2.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.

## New York — verified definition-based participant route

### Controlling current text

1. New York State Senate Open Legislation, Penal Law § 250.00, definitions:  
   https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/250.00
2. New York State Senate Open Legislation, Penal Law § 250.05, eavesdropping:  
   https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/250.05
3. New York State Senate Open Legislation, CPLR § 4506, eavesdropping evidence and suppression:  
   https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CVP/4506

Issuer: New York State Senate Open Legislation. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- Penal Law § 250.00(1) defines wiretapping as intentional overhearing or recording of telephonic or telegraphic communication by someone other than a sender or receiver, without either sender’s or receiver’s consent, using an instrument, device, or equipment.
- Penal Law § 250.00(2) defines mechanical overhearing as intentional device-assisted overhearing or recording by a person not present, without at least one party’s consent.
- Penal Law § 250.05 makes unlawful wiretapping, mechanical overhearing, or intercepting/accessing electronic communication eavesdropping.
- The definition-based exclusions support a bounded participant or one-party-authorized route for ordinary recording; they do not say that every third-party service, autonomous bot, downstream processor, or retained model input is the participant’s instrument.
- CPLR § 4506 excludes qualifying unlawfully obtained content and derivative evidence from specified proceedings and provides a suppression mechanism under its exact terms.
- The current Open Legislation page for CPLR § 4506 identifies its displayed revision date; a reliance-date check remains required.
- Separate privacy, employment, confidentiality, biometric, sector, records, privilege, contract, and disclosure rules remain outside this recording-layer finding.

### Bounded claim

Within New York’s recording layer, a participant recording the participant’s own communication, or a recording with at least one party’s consent within the statutory definitions, has a bounded route. This record does not decide whether a meeting bot is merely the participant’s instrument or a separate interceptor, and it does not authorize downstream processing.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.
