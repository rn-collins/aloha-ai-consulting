## Missouri — wire layer verified; oral layer unresolved

### Controlling current text

1. Missouri Revisor of Statutes, RSMo § 542.400, definitions:  
   https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=542.400
2. Missouri Revisor of Statutes, RSMo § 542.402, prohibition and permitted activities:  
   https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=542.402
3. Missouri Revisor of Statutes, RSMo § 542.418, civil action and remedies:  
   https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=542.418

Issuer: Missouri Revisor of Statutes. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08. The displayed current § 542.402 version is effective January 1, 2017.

### Pinpoint propositions

- Section 542.402.1 prohibits specified knowing interception of wire communications, specified device-assisted interception of oral communications, and knowing use or disclosure of unlawfully intercepted wire communications.
- Section 542.402.2(2) permits a person acting under law to intercept a wire or oral communication when that person is a party or one party gave prior consent.
- Section 542.402.2(3) permits a private person not acting under law to intercept a wire communication when that person is a party or one party gave prior consent, unless the purpose is criminal or tortious.
- The private-person clause in § 542.402.2(3) says “wire communication,” not “wire or oral communication.”
- Section 542.400 separately defines oral communication by reference to a justified expectation against interception and defines wire communication by use of wire, cable, or similar common-carrier facilities.
- Section 542.418 provides a civil cause of action and specified damages for a wire communication intercepted, disclosed, or used in violation of the statutory range, subject to its exact terms.
- The official annotation identifies *Lee v. Lee*, 967 S.W.2d 82 (Mo. App. W.D. 1998), for the proposition that a cellular-to-landline call was a wire communication within the statute.

### Bounded claim

Within Missouri’s wire-communication layer, a private person has a statutory route when the recorder is a party or one party gave prior consent, unless the prohibited-purpose limit applies. This record does not extend that route to every in-person or otherwise oral meeting, and it does not decide how modern internet meeting audio is classified under the current definitions.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.

## Montana — verified warning and knowledge framework

### Controlling current text

1. Montana Code Annotated 2025, § 45-8-213, Privacy in communications:  
   https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0450/chapter_0080/part_0020/section_0130/0450-0080-0020-0130.html

Issuer: Montana Legislative Services Division. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08. The official page identifies the 2025 MCA and records a 2025 amendment in the section history.

### Pinpoint propositions

- Section 45-8-213(1)(c) prohibits knowingly or purposely recording, or causing the recording of, a human conversation through a hidden electronic or mechanical device without the knowledge of all parties.
- Section 45-8-213(2)(a)(iii) excludes persons given warning of the transcription or recording and states that, if one person provides the warning, either party may record.
- Other narrow exclusions cover public officials or employees acting in official duty, people speaking at public meetings, and specified health-care emergency calls.
- Section 45-8-213(3) separately prohibits purposeful interception of an electronic communication, while excluding public officials or employees acting in official duty and persons given warning.
- Section 45-8-213(6)(b) defines electronic communication broadly across signs, signals, writing, images, sounds, data, or intelligence transmitted by specified systems.
- Section 45-8-213(4) supplies criminal consequences subject to the subsection and offense history.
- Knowledge or warning under this criminal provision is not, by itself, proof of informed, specific, accessible, voluntary, or noncoercive consent to vendor processing, transcription, summarization, retention, or disclosure.

### Bounded claim

Within Montana’s recording layer, hidden recording without all parties’ knowledge is prohibited. An express warning removes the hidden-recording bar identified in § 45-8-213, but this record does not treat silence, a passive bot icon, buried terms, or mere platform presence as proven warning or permission for downstream processing.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.

## Nebraska — verified recording layer

### Controlling current text

1. Nebraska Legislature, Neb. Rev. Stat. § 86-290, unlawful acts and exceptions:  
   https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=86-290
2. Nebraska Legislature, Neb. Rev. Stat. § 86-297, civil action:  
   https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=86-297
3. Nebraska Legislature, Chapter 86 index:  
   https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/browse-chapters.php?chapter=86

Issuer: Nebraska Unicameral Legislature. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- Section 86-290(1) prohibits intentional interception and specified knowing use or disclosure of intercepted wire, electronic, or oral communications.
- Section 86-290(2)(c) permits a private person not acting under color of law to intercept when that person is a party or one party gave prior consent.
- That route does not apply when the purpose is committing a criminal or tortious act under federal or state law.
- Section 86-290(2)(a) contains a separate employer/provider route for normal-course service or protection activities, but limits random monitoring and requires reasonable employee notice for the identified checks.
- The employer/provider language is not a general meeting-bot authorization and does not displace the need to evaluate participant status, purpose, power, vendor processing, or other legal layers.
- Section 86-297 supplies a civil action and forms of relief for qualifying violations, subject to its exact terms.
- The official annotation to § 86-290 identifies *White v. Longo*, 190 Neb. 703, 212 N.W.2d 84 (1973), concerning interception by a nonparty without prior consent.

### Bounded claim

Within Nebraska’s recording layer, a private person has a statutory route when the recorder is a party or one party gave prior consent, unless the prohibited-purpose limit applies. This does not resolve whether a meeting bot is the participant’s instrument, a separate interceptor, or a vendor processing service under the exact architecture.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.

## Nevada — verified medium-specific split

### Controlling current authority

1. Nevada Legislature, NRS Chapter 200, §§ 200.610–200.690:  
   https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-200.html#NRS200Sec610
2. Nevada Supreme Court, *Ditech Financial LLC v. Buckles*, 133 Nev. 498, 401 P.3d 103 (2017), discussing *Lane v. Allstate Insurance Co.*, 114 Nev. 1176, 969 P.2d 938 (1998):  
   https://nvcourts.gov/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/11467/133_nevada_reports_pages_438-500.pdf
3. Nevada Legislature, 2007 enacted-law digest describing the existing two-party telephone rule and *Lane*:  
   https://www.leg.state.nv.us/statutes/74th/Stats200720.html

Issuers: Nevada Legislature and Supreme Court of Nevada. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- NRS 200.620(1) prohibits interception of wire communications unless one party gave prior consent and an emergency makes a court order impractical, subject to later judicial ratification, or another specific exception applies.
- The Nevada Supreme Court in *Lane*, as restated in *Ditech*, construed NRS 200.620 to prohibit recording telephone conversations with only one party’s consent.
- *Ditech* also holds that NRS 200.620 does not govern an interception made outside Nevada, making the location of interception a material multistate fact.
- NRS 200.650 separately prohibits surreptitious device-assisted intrusion into private conversations of others unless one person in the conversation authorizes it.
- NRS 200.630 separately restricts disclosure of wire or radio communications unless authorized by sender or receiver or another exact exception.
- NRS 200.690 supplies criminal and civil consequences for willful and knowing violations, including actual or liquidated damages, punitive damages, costs, and attorney’s fees under its terms.
- The difference between telephone or wire interception and private in-person conversation prevents a safe statewide shorthand label for remote, hybrid, or platform-routed meetings.

### Bounded claim

For an ordinary telephone or wire-recorded meeting intercepted in Nevada, documented prior consent from every participant is the conservative rule supported by current statute and controlling construction. For a private in-person conversation, NRS 200.650 contains a one-participant authorization route. Until the communication medium, interception location, participants, and system architecture are frozen, the project must default to documented all-participant consent without claiming that Nevada always requires it.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.
