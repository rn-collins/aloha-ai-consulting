Status: open governed record for authority packet `MCAP-1.0`.

## Current boundary

- The method, 51-row inventory, source hierarchy, federal baseline register, and cross-border issue map are complete as research infrastructure.
- Six jurisdictions—Hawaiʻi, California, Massachusetts, New York, New Jersey, and Florida—have verified recording-authority layers; zero jurisdiction packets are fully verified or cleared. No row may be shortened to a universal party-consent label or treated as meeting-capture authority because six required layers, the exact configuration, participant locations, and conflict analysis remain open. - Federal participant-consent language is not a nationwide operational clearance. Stricter state law, different definitions, confidentiality/privacy rules, labor law, privilege, records duties, sector law, accessibility obligations, contracts, and foreign law may independently prohibit or condition capture and later processing.
- Consent to recording is not consent to transcription, summarization, model processing, search, export, retention, training, voiceprint extraction, automated decisions, or publication.
- A visible bot, calendar notice, continued attendance, or a host's consent does not by itself prove valid consent for every participant or later use.
- Location and conflict-of-law analysis remain unresolved whenever participants, organization, vendor, storage, support access, or later use span jurisdictions.

## Florida-specific limitation

Florida Statutes §§ 934.02 and 934.03 support a bounded all-parties-prior-consent rule only after the communication, device, acquisition, expectation, purpose, and applicable exception are classified. McDade confirms that protected oral-communication status turns on the surrounding circumstances, and § 934.10 adds civil exposure and defenses. None of these authorities proves consent to transcription, summarization, model processing, retention, disclosure, workplace monitoring, or multistate capture, and the exact facts and later authority must be rechecked for an operational packet.

## New Jersey-specific limitation

N.J.S.A. 2A:156A-4(d) currently supports a bounded participant-or-one-party-prior-consent classification for a private actor, subject to criminal, tortious, or other injurious-purpose limits. Sections 2A:156A-2 and -3 require separate analysis of communication type, device acquisition, justified expectation against interception, purpose, disclosure, and use; section 2A:156A-24 adds civil exposure. Stark is unpublished and turns on specific workplace facts, so it does not establish universal employer authority. Assembly Bill 6186 proposes an all-parties rule but is not enacted law; enactment, amendment, or a successor bill is an immediate reopening trigger. None of these authorities clears labor, privilege, records, privacy, accessibility, transcription, model processing, retention, disclosure, or multistate capture.

## New York-specific limitation

Penal Law § 250.00 distinguishes wiretapping, mechanical overhearing, and interception or access of electronic communications; recorder participation, presence, communication type, device use, consent, and statutory authorization therefore matter. Section 250.05 criminalizes unlawful eavesdropping, while CPLR 4506 can exclude unlawfully obtained communications and derivative evidence. Locke confirms participant recording under New York law but applies a conflicts analysis to interstate calls involving California. None of these authorities establishes that New York law governs every multistate meeting or supplies permission for workplace monitoring, transcription, summarization, model processing, retention, disclosure, or publication.

## Massachusetts-specific limitation

General Laws c. 272, § 99 turns statutory interception on secret hearing or recording without prior authority from all parties; Curtatone confirms that actual knowledge defeats secrecy even without affirmative consent. Project Veritas creates a narrow First Amendment limit for secret, nonconsensual audio recording of police officers performing official duties in public spaces, not a general meeting-recording exception. Actual knowledge, device use, participant identity and location, setting, interference, statutory exceptions, and later use remain fact-specific. None of these authorities establishes valid workplace choice or permission for transcription, summarization, model processing, retention, or disclosure.

## California-specific limitation

Penal Code § 632 requires all-party consent only for a “confidential communication” as statutorily defined, while § 632.7 separately addresses specified communications involving cellular or cordless telephones. Smith establishes that § 632.7 reaches participant recorders but did not decide on remand whether a beep followed by continued participation proved consent. Kearney confirms that California’s privacy interest may matter in interstate business calls involving a California participant, but it requires choice-of-law analysis rather than a universal location rule. None of those authorities proves consent to transcription, summarization, model processing, retention, or later use.

## Hawaiʻi-specific limitation

HRS § 803-42(b)(3)(A) supports a bounded participant/prior-consent classification for a private actor, subject to its criminal-or-tortious-purpose limitation. That proposition does not establish valid employee consent, permission for transcription or model processing, privilege preservation, records treatment, accessibility sufficiency, cross-border transfer authority, or which law governs a multistate meeting. The official 2026 Aquino-Martinez disposition concerns a law-enforcement participant under subsection (b)(4) and is not a precedential private-workplace clearance.
