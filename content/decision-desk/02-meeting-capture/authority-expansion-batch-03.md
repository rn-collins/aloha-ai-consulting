### Pinpoint propositions

- Section 934.02(1)–(3) separately defines wire communication, protected oral communication, and interception.
- Section 934.02(2) limits protected oral communication to speech uttered with a justified expectation against interception and excludes public oral communication at a public meeting.
- Section 934.02(3) defines interception as the aural or other acquisition of communication contents through an electronic, mechanical, or other device.
- Section 934.03(1) prohibits intentional interception and specified knowing use or disclosure, except as the chapter provides.
- Section 934.03(2)(d) makes interception lawful when **all parties** gave prior consent.
- Section 934.03(2)(c) supplies a narrower one-party route for specified law-enforcement evidence gathering; it is not a general workplace or commercial-meeting exception.
- Section 934.03(4) establishes criminal consequences, subject to its exact classifications and exceptions.
- Section 934.06 addresses use of unlawfully intercepted wire or oral communications as evidence.
- Section 934.10 supplies a civil cause of action and specified relief for unlawful interception, disclosure, or use.

### Bounded claim

For an ordinary private meeting within Florida's recording layer, the safe statutory rule is documented prior consent from every party before interception. The exact communication type, justified expectation, identities and locations of all participants, purpose, consent evidence, exceptions, downstream processing, and every separate legal layer must still be resolved.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.
