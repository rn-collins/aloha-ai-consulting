# Issue 02 editorial evidence reconciliation

Status: draft evidence record; refreshed 2026-08-08

## Governing public conclusion

An organization should not turn on AI capture for every meeting. It should first define one meeting class, identify the people and power relationships involved, review every applicable authority layer, separate the records the system creates, freeze one exact product configuration, and test only with fictional data. Until those gates close, the defensible outcome is **human notes only** or **do not capture**.

This is a governance recommendation, not a universal legal conclusion. Zero jurisdictions are represented as fully cleared. No product has been selected or tested.

## Claims narrowed or removed

- Removed unsupported assertions that capture necessarily makes people speak less freely or move work into side channels. Those are plausible research questions, not established findings in the current record.
- Removed any suggestion that notice, a beep, a bot tile, or continued attendance is always legally insufficient. The article now says none should be treated as universal proof because sufficiency depends on the law and facts.
- Removed any implication that the seven partially researched states are cleared.
- Removed product capability, accuracy, privacy, or security claims because no exact product and configuration have been frozen.
- Removed universal claims about consent. Recording permission, organizational authority, accessibility, labor, confidentiality, later use, and meaningful choice are treated as separate questions.

## Product disposition

Vendor silence or an unfreezeable configuration resolves to no-product finding, not inference.
