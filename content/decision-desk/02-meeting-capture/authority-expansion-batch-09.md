## North Carolina — verified one-party statutory floor

### Controlling current text

1. North Carolina General Assembly, G.S. 15A-287, Interception and disclosure of wire, oral, or electronic communications prohibited:  
   https://ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_15A/GS_15A-287.html
2. North Carolina General Assembly, G.S. 15A-296, Recovery of civil damages authorized:  
   https://www.ncleg.gov/enactedlegislation/statutes/html/bysection/chapter_15a/gs_15a-296.html
3. North Carolina General Assembly, Article 16, Electronic Surveillance:  
   https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByArticle/Chapter_15A/Article_16.html
4. North Carolina General Assembly, Chapter 15A section index and later-history indicators:  
   https://www.ncleg.gov/Laws/GeneralStatuteSections/Chapter15A

Issuer: North Carolina General Assembly. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-09.

### Pinpoint propositions

- G.S. 15A-287(a) makes a person guilty of a Class H felony when, without at least one party’s consent and outside a specific exception, the person willfully intercepts, attempts or procures interception, or knowingly uses or discloses qualifying contents.
- The statutory floor is at least one party’s consent; it does not establish that a vendor, autonomous bot, downstream processor, or retained model input is merely the consenting participant’s instrument.
- G.S. 15A-286 defines covered communication and interception terms; the actual device, transmission path, acquisition point, and privacy expectation still require classification.
- G.S. 15A-296 supplies a civil cause of action and identifies actual or liquidated damages, punitive damages, reasonable attorney’s fees, and litigation costs under its terms.
- The official chapter index identifies 2026 later history in Article 16. Even where the displayed amendment marker concerns another section, all relevant later history must be checked through the reliance date.
- Consent to interception does not establish permission for transcription, summarization, extraction, retention, training, disclosure, publication, or personnel use.

### Bounded claim

Within North Carolina’s recording layer, at least one party’s consent supplies the statutory floor for ordinary interception under G.S. 15A-287. This finding does not resolve participant agency, product architecture, downstream processing, workplace authority, or multistate conflicts.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.

## North Dakota — verified defense-based participant route

### Controlling current text

1. North Dakota Legislative Branch, N.D.C.C. ch. 12.1-15, Defamation — Interception of Communications:  
   https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t12-1c15.pdf
2. North Dakota Legislative Branch, official Century Code index:  
   https://ndlegis.gov/general-information/north-dakota-century-code/index.html

Issuer: North Dakota Legislative Branch. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-09.

### Pinpoint propositions

- N.D.C.C. § 12.1-15-02(1) makes intentional device-assisted interception, and specified knowing use or disclosure of intercepted wire or oral communications, a class C felony.
- Section 12.1-15-02(3)(c) supplies a defense when the actor is a party or one party gave prior consent and the communication was not intercepted to commit a crime or other unlawful harm.
- Because consent appears as a defense rather than as an element exclusion, the record should preserve that procedural distinction.
- Section 12.1-15-04 limits “oral communication” to an utterance made with an expectation against interception under circumstances justifying that expectation.
- The displayed chapter text does not itself resolve electronic-only communications, third-party cloud acquisition, participant agency, or downstream processing.
- A reliance-date check remains required for amendments, controlling construction, remedies, and the fit between modern meeting architecture and the statute’s wire/oral categories.

### Bounded claim

North Dakota provides a bounded defense for a participant or a person with one party’s prior consent when the interception is not for a crime or other unlawful harm. This is not universal permission for a meeting bot or later uses of captured material.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.

## Ohio — verified express private-person exception

### Controlling current text

1. Ohio Laws and Administrative Rules, Revised Code Chapter 2933:  
   https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-2933
2. Ohio Revised Code § 2933.52, Interception of wire, oral or electronic communications:  
   https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2933.52
3. Ohio Revised Code § 2933.65, Civil and criminal actions for wiretapping and electronic-surveillance violations:  
   https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2933.65

Issuer: Ohio Laws and Administrative Rules. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-09.

### Pinpoint propositions

- R.C. 2933.52(A) prohibits purposeful interception, specified device use, and knowing use of contents obtained in violation of the statutory scheme.
- Section 2933.52(B)(4) expressly excludes a private person who is a party or has one party’s prior consent, provided the communication is not intercepted to commit a criminal offense, tortious act, or other injurious act.
- The purpose limitation must remain part of the rule; “one-party state” alone is incomplete.
- Section 2933.52(C) classifies a violation as a fourth-degree felony.
- R.C. 2933.65 provides equitable relief, liquidated or actual/profit-based damages, possible punitive damages, reasonable attorney’s fees, litigation expenses, a two-year discovery-based limitations rule, and specified defenses under its terms.
- The official page reports § 2933.52’s effective date and latest legislation, but a reliance-date later-history and controlling-case review remains required.

### Bounded claim

Within Ohio’s recording layer, a private participant or a person with one party’s prior consent has an express route when the interception is not for a criminal, tortious, or other injurious purpose. This does not authorize an unclassified third-party bot or downstream processing.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.

## Oklahoma — verified text with mandatory currency check

### Official text located

1. Oklahoma Senate, Oklahoma Statutes Title 13, Common Carriers, including §§ 176.2–176.4:  
   https://oksenate.gov/sites/default/files/2019-12/os13.pdf
2. Oklahoma State Courts Network / Official Oklahoma Statutes (Unannotated), Security of Communications Act table of contents:  
   https://govt.westlaw.com/okjc/Browse/Home/Oklahoma/OfficialOklahomaStatutes?guid=N6F7A0E80C37D11DB8F04FB3E68C8F4C5

Issuer: Oklahoma Senate and Oklahoma State Courts Network official-statutes service. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-09. The Senate PDF URL and document do not establish a current compilation date; a reliance-date later-history refresh is mandatory.

### Pinpoint propositions

- Title 13 § 176.3 states the core prohibited interception, use, and disclosure acts under the Security of Communications Act.
- Section 176.4(5) states that it is not unlawful for a person not acting under color of law to intercept a wire, oral, or electronic communication when the person is a party or one party gave prior consent, unless the purpose is committing a criminal act.
- The criminal-purpose limit is part of the exception and must not be omitted.
- The statutory definitions, acquisition point, covered medium, product role, and participant agency still require classification for a web-conference or cloud-routed bot.
- The official compiled PDF does not itself prove that no later amendment affects §§ 176.2–176.4.
- Before any operational reliance, retrieve current section-level text, verify session-law history through the reliance date, and review controlling decisions and remedies.

### Bounded claim

Oklahoma’s official compiled text supports a participant or one-party-prior-consent route for a private person, subject to a criminal-purpose limit. Because the compilation date is not established on the retrieved PDF, this finding cannot become an operational clearance without a current later-history check.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.
