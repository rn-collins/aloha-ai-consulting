## Kentucky — verified recording layer

### Controlling current text

1. Kentucky General Assembly, KRS Chapter 526, including enactments through the 2026 Regular Session:  
   https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/chapter.aspx?id=39393
2. KRS 526.010, definition:  
   https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=19948
3. KRS 526.020, eavesdropping:  
   https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=19949
4. KRS 526.070, exceptions:  
   https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=19954

Issuer: Kentucky General Assembly. The chapter states that it includes enactments through the 2026 Regular Session and that the database was updated on 2026-08-08. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- KRS 526.010 defines “eavesdrop” as using an electronic, mechanical, or other device to overhear, record, amplify, or transmit a wire or oral communication of others without at least one party's consent.
- KRS 526.020 makes intentional use of a device to eavesdrop a Class D felony, whether or not the person is present.
- KRS 526.070 supplies narrow exceptions for inadvertent party-line or extension overhearing without divulgence and specified common-carrier activity.
- Those narrow exceptions do not provide general permission for an ordinary workplace or commercial meeting bot.

### Bounded claim

Within Kentucky's recording layer, device-assisted capture does not meet Chapter 526's definition of eavesdropping when at least one party consents. Application still requires the exact communication, recorder, device behavior, participant locations, consent record, purpose, later use and disclosure, and every separate legal layer.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.

## Louisiana — verified recording layer

### Controlling current text

1. Louisiana Legislature, R.S. 15:1303, interception and disclosure of wire, electronic, or oral communications:  
   https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=78938

Issuer: Louisiana Legislature. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- R.S. 15:1303(A) prohibits willful interception and specified knowing use or disclosure of unlawfully intercepted wire, electronic, or oral communications.
- R.S. 15:1303(B) supplies criminal penalties.
- R.S. 15:1303(C)(4) provides that a private person does not violate the chapter when that person is a party or one party gave prior consent.
- That private-person route does not apply when the interception is for a criminal or tortious act or another injurious act.
- R.S. 15:1303(D)(4) states that interception does not strip an otherwise privileged communication of its privileged character.
- The section contains additional narrow provider, regulatory, emergency, and law-enforcement provisions that do not establish general permission for an ordinary meeting bot.

### Bounded claim

Within Louisiana's recording layer, a private person may have a statutory route when the interceptor is a party or one party gave prior consent, unless the prohibited-purpose limitation applies. That does not resolve privilege, confidentiality, participant power, multistate choice of law, vendor processing, retention, or later disclosure.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.

## Maine — verified recording layer with definition-based boundaries

### Controlling current text

1. 15 M.R.S. § 709, definitions:  
   https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/statutes/15/title15sec709.html
2. 15 M.R.S. § 710, offenses:  
   https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/statutes/15/title15sec710.html
3. 15 M.R.S. § 711, civil remedy:  
   https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/statutes/15/title15sec711.html

Issuer: Maine Legislature, Office of the Revisor of Statutes. The pages state that their data were extracted on 2026-01-05. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- Section 709(4) defines “intercept” as device-assisted hearing or recording by a person other than the sender, receiver, someone within normal unaided hearing range, or a person given prior authority by the sender or receiver.
- Section 709(5) defines oral communications by reference to an exhibited expectation against interception under circumstances justifying that expectation.
- Section 710(1) makes intentional or knowing interception or attempted interception a Class C crime, subject to its stated institutional exceptions.
- Section 710(3) separately criminalizes intentional or knowing disclosure or use when the person knows the information was obtained through interception.
- Section 711 provides a civil cause of action, actual or minimum liquidated damages, and reasonable attorney's fees and litigation disbursements.
- The statutory structure means “one-party consent” is only shorthand. Sender/receiver status, prior authority, hearing range, the device, and the justified privacy expectation all matter.

### Bounded claim

Within Maine's recording layer, the sender, receiver, or a person given prior authority by either one is outside the statutory definition of “intercept.” An ordinary meeting workflow still must resolve whether the communication is protected, who operates the device, participant locations, valid authority, use and disclosure, and every separate legal layer.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.

## Maryland — verified recording layer

### Controlling current text

1. Maryland General Assembly, Courts and Judicial Proceedings § 10-402:  
   https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/laws/StatuteText?article=gcj&section=10-402
2. Maryland General Assembly, Courts and Judicial Proceedings § 10-410, civil recovery:  
   https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/laws/StatuteText?article=gcj&section=10-410

Issuer: Maryland General Assembly. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- Section 10-402(a) prohibits willful interception and specified knowing disclosure or use of unlawfully intercepted wire, oral, or electronic communications.
- Section 10-402(b) supplies a felony penalty of up to five years, a fine of up to $10,000, or both.
- Section 10-402(c)(3) provides the ordinary private-person route only when the interceptor is a party and all parties gave prior consent.
- That route does not apply when interception is for a criminal or tortious purpose.
- The section contains narrower law-enforcement, emergency, public-system, provider, and other exceptions; they are not general permission for workplace or commercial meeting capture.
- Section 10-410 provides civil recovery subject to its exact terms.
- Because 2026 legislation addressed intercepted communications, final enrolled laws and effective dates must be checked again before operational reliance.

### Bounded claim

For an ordinary private meeting within Maryland's recording layer, the safe statutory rule is documented prior consent from every party, with the recorder also a party. This does not establish that notice alone, silence, a vendor join message, or a platform setting is valid consent, and it does not resolve any other legal or organizational layer.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.
