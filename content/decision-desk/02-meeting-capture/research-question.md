# Research question

When, for what meeting classes, and under what legal, labor, privacy, security, records, accessibility, cultural, and human-behavior conditions should an organization permit AI transcription or summarization?

## Required research modules

1. Recording and consent law by actual jurisdiction.
2. Employment, labor, protected activity, monitoring, and collective-agreement constraints.
3. Privilege, public-records, discovery, retention, sector, cross-border, and special-category data issues.
4. Vendor/product data flows and administrative controls by exact configuration and version.
5. Accuracy across speakers, accents, languages, audio quality, attribution, interruption, and domain vocabulary.
6. Behavioral effects: withholding, chilling, side-channel migration, power, consent quality, and relationship-centered work.
7. Record taxonomy, access, correction, deletion, and authoritative decision record.
8. Stakeholder perspectives, including workers, disabled participants, community participants, counsel, security, and records owners.

## Disconfirmation question

What evidence shows that even a named meeting class should remain uncaptured?

## Governed authority infrastructure

The `MCAP-1.0` authority-packet method, 51-jurisdiction inventory, federal/cross-border source and claim registers, search log, and contradictions record are complete as research infrastructure. They clear zero jurisdictions and authorize no participant capture. Each row requires current controlling sources, pinpoints, independent verification, implicated-layer review, and a packet-specific qualified reviewer before it can advance.
