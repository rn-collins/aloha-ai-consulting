## Illinois — verified recording layer

### Controlling current text

1. 720 ILCS 5/14-1, definitions:  
   https://www.ilga.gov/documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/072000050K14-1.htm
2. 720 ILCS 5/14-2, elements and defenses:  
   https://www.ilga.gov/documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/072000050K14-2.htm

Issuer: Illinois General Assembly. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- Section 14-1 defines an eavesdropping device broadly enough to include a device capable of hearing or recording oral conversation.
- Section 14-1 defines a private conversation as an oral communication between two or more persons when one or more parties intended privacy under circumstances reasonably justifying that expectation.
- Section 14-2(a)(1) reaches surreptitious use of an eavesdropping device to record a private conversation to which the recorder is not a party unless all parties consent.
- Section 14-2(a)(2) reaches surreptitious recording by a party unless all other parties consent.
- Section 14-2(a)(3) separately addresses surreptitious interception, recording, or transcription of private electronic communications.
- Section 14-2 includes narrower statutory exceptions and defenses that are not general permission for an ordinary workplace or commercial meeting.

### Bounded claim

For a private conversation within Illinois's recording layer, an ordinary meeting-capture workflow should not record surreptitiously and should obtain documented consent from every participant before capture. This is not a claim that Illinois requires all-party consent for every public, nonprivate, or nonsurreptitious communication.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.

## Iowa — verified recording layer

### Controlling current text

1. Iowa Code 2026, Chapter 808B, official consolidated PDF:  
   https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/808B.pdf

Issuer: Iowa Legislature. The official PDF bears a 2026 Code timestamp. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- Section 808B.1(6) defines interception as the aural acquisition of communication contents through an electronic, mechanical, or other device.
- Section 808B.1(8) protects oral communication when the speaker exhibits an expectation against interception under circumstances justifying it.
- Section 808B.2(1) prohibits willful interception and specified knowing disclosure or use, with a class D felony consequence.
- Section 808B.2(2)(c) permits a person not acting under color of law to intercept when that person is a party or one party gave prior consent.
- The same private-party exception does not apply when interception is for a criminal or tortious act or another injurious act.
- Section 808B.7 limits evidentiary use of unlawfully intercepted content.
- Section 808B.8 authorizes civil damages and injunctive relief, subject to its exact terms and defenses.

### Bounded claim

Within Iowa's recording layer, a private person may have a statutory route when the recorder is a party or one party gave prior consent, unless the prohibited-purpose limitation applies. The exact communication, expectations, participant locations, purpose, consent record, use, disclosure, and other legal layers must still be resolved.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.

## Kansas — verified recording layer with overlapping clauses

### Controlling current text

1. K.S.A. 21-6101, 2026 Kansas Statutes, breach of privacy:  
   https://kslegislature.gov/b2025_26/laws/021_000_0000_chapter/021_061_0000_article/021_061_0001_section/021_061_0001_k/
2. Kansas 2025–2026 legislation index for HB 2518, later-history review:  
   https://www.kslegislature.gov/b2025_26/bills/HB2518/

Issuer: Kansas Legislature. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- Section 21-6101(a)(1) addresses interception of a private message without consent of the sender or receiver.
- Section 21-6101(a)(3) addresses surreptitious listening to private conversations in a private place.
- Section 21-6101(a)(4) addresses installing or using a device to hear, record, amplify, or broadcast otherwise inaudible or incomprehensible sounds from a private place without consent of the person or persons entitled to privacy there.
- Section 21-6101(a)(5) separately addresses interception equipment for wire or wireless communication without consent of the person possessing or controlling the facilities.
- Section 21-6101(b) supplies criminal classifications for the relevant clauses.
- The text contains specific exceptions that do not establish general permission for an ordinary meeting bot.
- The 2026 legislative history was reviewed because bills in the biennium touched § 21-6101; application still requires a final effective-date and later-history check at reliance time.

### Bounded claim

Kansas cannot responsibly be reduced to a simple one-party label for AI meeting capture. A private-message clause may be satisfied by sender-or-receiver consent, while a device capturing sounds from a private place implicates consent of the person or persons entitled to privacy. Until the exact medium, place, device behavior, participants, and controlling clause are resolved, the safe operational default is documented consent from every person entitled to privacy.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`. No meeting bot, recording, transcription, or product test is authorized.
