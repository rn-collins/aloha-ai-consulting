## Colorado — verified recording layer

### Controlling current text

1. Colorado Revised Statutes 2025, Title 18, §§ 18-9-301 and 18-9-303 to -305:  
   https://olls.info/crs/crs2025-title-18.htm
2. Colorado General Assembly, 2025 CRS titles and currency statement:  
   https://content.leg.colorado.gov/agencies/office-legislative-legal-services/2025-crs-titles-download

Issuer: Colorado General Assembly, Office of Legislative Legal Services. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- Section 18-9-303(1) applies to a person who is not a sender or intended receiver of a telephone or telegraph communication.
- Section 18-9-303(1)(a) addresses knowingly overhearing, reading, taking, copying, or recording a telephone, telegraph, or electronic communication without consent of either a sender or receiver.
- Section 18-9-304(1) applies to a person not visibly present during a conversation or discussion.
- Section 18-9-304(1)(a) addresses knowingly overhearing or recording without consent of at least one principal party.
- Section 18-9-304(1)(b) separately reaches intentional overhearing or recording for the purpose of committing, aiding, or abetting an unlawful act.
- Section 18-9-305 contains specific exceptions and affirmative defenses, including a reasonable-notice rule for certain security or business uses on one's own premises; those provisions are not a universal meeting-bot authorization.
- The official 2025 compilation is current through the August 2025 First Extraordinary Session. The General Assembly states that 2026 legislation enacted without a safety clause generally takes effect August 12, 2026, creating a mandatory near-term later-history trigger.

### Bounded claim

Colorado's current statutory recording layer generally permits a sender, intended receiver, visibly present participant, or person acting with at least one principal party's consent to avoid the core wiretapping/eavesdropping prohibitions. The exact communication type, participant status, visibility/presence, purpose, consent evidence, exceptions, and 2026 later history must still be resolved.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`.

## Delaware — verified conflict and conservative operational rule

### Controlling current text

1. 11 Del. C. §§ 2401–2402, Electronic Surveillance and Interception of Communications:  
   https://delcode.delaware.gov/title11/c024/sc01/
2. 11 Del. C. § 1335, Violation of Privacy:  
   https://delcode.delaware.gov/title11/c005/sc07/#1335

Issuer: Delaware General Assembly, Delaware Code Online. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- Section 2401(10) defines “intercept” as aural or other acquisition of communication contents through an electronic, mechanical, or other device.
- Section 2401(13) limits “oral communication” to speech made with a justified expectation against interception.
- Section 2402(a) prohibits intentional interception and certain knowing disclosure or use.
- Section 2402(c)(4) makes interception lawful when the interceptor is a party or one party gave prior consent, unless the purpose is a criminal or tortious act.
- Section 1335(a)(4) separately prohibits intercepting, without all parties' consent, a message by telephone, telegraph, letter, or other private means, including private conversation.
- Section 1335(a)(5) separately regulates divulgence of unlawfully intercepted private messages.
- The provisions carry different classifications and exceptions; neither may be silently discarded.

### Bounded claim

Delaware's current statutory text contains a material consent-rule tension. A meeting system must use documented all-party consent as the operational minimum unless qualified Delaware review resolves the statutes, controlling case law, communication type, participant role, purpose, and facts differently. “All-party operational default” is a risk-control decision, not a claim that every Delaware court would characterize every recording the same way.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`; no bot or recording test is authorized.

## District of Columbia — verified recording layer

### Controlling current text

1. D.C. Code § 23-541, definitions:  
   https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/23-541
2. D.C. Code § 23-542, interception, disclosure, and use prohibited:  
   https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/23-542

Issuer: Council of the District of Columbia, D.C. Law Library. Retrieval and verification date: 2026-08-08.

### Pinpoint propositions

- Section 23-541 defines wire communication, oral communication, intercept, and related terms; the definition of oral communication turns on a justified expectation against interception.
- Section 23-542(a) prohibits willful interception and certain knowing disclosure or use of wire or oral communications.
- Section 23-542(b)(2) provides a party-or-one-party-prior-consent path for a person acting under color of law.
- Section 23-542(b)(3) provides the same basic path for a person not acting under color of law, unless the communication is intercepted for a criminal, tortious, or other injurious act.
- The statute does not itself answer downstream transcription, summarization, model processing, retention, disclosure, publication, employment-power, privilege, accessibility, or multistate questions.

### Bounded claim

The District's current statutory recording layer generally supplies a participant/one-party-prior-consent path, subject to covered-communication definitions, location and governing-law analysis, purpose, consent evidence, and other legal layers.

Disposition remains `human_notes_only`.
