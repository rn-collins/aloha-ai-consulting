# Decision Desk inaugural curriculum coverage review

Status: governed selection record; issue selection only, not research completion or release authorization  
Owner: RN Collins / Aloha AI  
Decision date: 2026-08-07  
Review trigger: before any launch authorization and whenever a material coverage assumption changes

## Decision

The inaugural Decision Desk curriculum is a twelve-issue monthly sequence. The first three issues remain the seed drafts; Issues 04–12 are selected research questions, not drafted or publishable products.

Selecting twelve issues closes only the curriculum-selection gate. Every issue must still independently satisfy the Polymath editorial research flywheel and reach R7. No issue may borrow another issue's research grade, evidence, visual clearance, tool completion, or release approval.

## What the inaugural set must teach

By the end of the sequence, a subscriber should be able to:

1. decide whether a proposed AI use belongs in the work at all;
2. identify the decision owner, affected people, authoritative record, and nondelegable human judgment;
3. classify data, authority, consequence, reversibility, and escalation risk before selecting a product;
4. distinguish retrieval, summarization, generation, classification, recommendation, and autonomous action;
5. evaluate a named product and configuration rather than infer safety from a product category;
6. design a bounded predeployment test with representative inputs, error classes, stop rules, and accountable reviewers;
7. preserve notice, consent, contestability, accessibility, privacy, security, records, and professional obligations;
8. procure and contract for the actual data flow, vendors, subprocessors, retention, training use, access, and exit path;
9. monitor production performance, incidents, workarounds, distributional effects, and model or policy drift; and
10. pause, roll back, replace, or retire a system when evidence no longer supports continued use.

## Coverage method

Candidates were retained only when they added a materially different combination of:

- work stage: intake, capture, knowledge, drafting, decision support, transaction, or action;
- AI role: retrieve, summarize, classify, generate, recommend, or execute;
- consequence: informational, relational, financial, employment, legal, health, safety, or rights-affecting;
- evidence problem: authority, product behavior, human factors, data provenance, fairness, accessibility, security, or operations;
- decision owner: professional, manager, small-business operator, product/procurement owner, educator, or executive; and
- reusable artifact: evaluation card, authority map, test suite, procurement record, escalation design, incident ledger, or retirement plan.

A candidate was excluded or deferred when it substantially duplicated another issue, depended on individualized advice, was primarily a product tutorial, could not yield a reusable decision tool, or required a breadth of sector-specific authority that could not be responsibly bounded in one issue.

## Sequence logic

- Issues 01–03 begin with familiar, bounded work decisions and expose three distinct AI roles: summarization, ambient capture, and customer-facing generation.
- Issues 04–07 move from internal knowledge to decisions and public representations, making authority, evidence, access, and consequence progressively more explicit.
- Issues 08–10 address identity, autonomous action, and vendor dependency—the points at which an AI choice begins binding other people or the organization itself.
- Issues 11–12 close the cycle with developmentally consequential use and the institutional capacity to monitor, stop, repair, and exit.

The sequence is pedagogical, not a release schedule. An issue may not publish merely because its month arrives. If a later issue reaches R7 first, release order still requires an explicit owner decision and a documented explanation of any sequence change.

## Coverage findings and intentional limits

The set covers the full operating lifecycle from problem framing through retirement, every major AI role, internal and external uses, individual and institutional decisions, and multiple consequence classes. It deliberately does not promise exhaustive treatment of every industry or jurisdiction.

The following remain cross-cutting requirements for every issue rather than standalone substitutes for issue-level research:

- privacy, security, accessibility, records, rights, labor, and professional-duty review where applicable;
- product- and configuration-specific evidence;
- affected-person and frontline-worker evidence;
- counterevidence, incident, complaint, and enforcement searches;
- documentary visual evidence and rights clearance;
- a testable tool, teaching assets, clinic translation, and change-watch plan; and
- claim-level freshness, fact-checking, rendering, delivery, and owner approval.

## Deferred candidates

These questions are useful but do not add enough inaugural coverage to displace a selected issue:

- coding copilots, because their core authority, verification, security, and action-control questions are represented in Issues 04, 09, and 10;
- general “AI strategy,” because it is a method spanning the sequence rather than a bounded decision question;
- model-versus-model comparisons, because named products and configurations belong inside each relevant issue and become stale quickly;
- sector-wide legal surveys, because the unit of work must remain a bounded decision with an identified audience and jurisdiction; and
- individualized tool recommendations, because Decision Desk is editorial decision intelligence, not private consulting.

## Reopening triggers

Reopen this selection decision if any of the following occurs before launch:

- a material lifecycle stage, AI role, affected group, or consequence class is shown to be absent;
- two selected issues become substantially duplicative after research;
- an issue cannot be bounded to a supportable research question and reusable tool;
- primary evidence reveals that an issue must be split to avoid misleading generalization;
- the subscriber audience or delivery model materially changes; or
- RN directs a different inaugural thesis or sequence.
