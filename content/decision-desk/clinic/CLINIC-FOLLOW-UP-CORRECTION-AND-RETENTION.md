# Clinic follow-up, correction, privacy, and retention protocol

Status: draft; inactive  
Version: 0.1

## Participant output

Within the published service level, provide:

1. the bounded decision record;
2. the participant’s workflow map;
3. evidence gaps and unverified assumptions;
4. safeguards and stop conditions;
5. assigned next actions and review date;
6. the generic Decision Desk resources promised at purchase;
7. participant-authored if–then action, owner, stop authority, and review trigger;
8. material dissent or unresolved alternative.

Do not include facilitator-only notes, other participants’ information, sensitive details that were interrupted, sealed answers, or unsupported conclusions.

## Correction window

- Invite factual corrections through a defined, accessible channel.
- Acknowledge within the published support target.
- Distinguish factual correction from a request to change the disposition without new evidence.
- Version every revised deliverable.
- Record what changed, why, who approved it, and the revision date.
- If an error could affect other participant materials, assess and correct those materials without revealing the reporting participant.
- A participant may request clarification without purchasing advisory services.

## Implementation and maintenance follow-up

Ask whether the named owner accepted the record, the first action occurred, any stop/defect appeared, and the planned review happened. Nonresponse is unknown, not success. Attendance, satisfaction, confidence, and worksheet completion are not adoption or sustainment. A defect, exception, access barrier, changed authority/configuration, or affected-person harm must route through the recorded stop/review mechanism and may reopen the decision.

## Follow-up boundary

The standard clinic includes one clarification/correction cycle. New research, vendor diligence, product testing, legal analysis, implementation, procurement, policy drafting, or organizational facilitation requires a separately scoped engagement. Follow-up communications may describe that option but must not pressure participants or imply that their clinic result is incomplete without it.

Marketing contact requires separate opt-in consent.

## Minimum retention design

Before collection begins, publish and configure exact periods for:

- transaction and tax records;
- fit-screen outcome;
- contact and accessibility logistics;
- attendance record;
- participant deliverable;
- correction log;
- optional feedback;
- consent evidence.

Do not retain raw sensitive disclosures. Do not record the initial release. Delete rejected intake descriptions promptly after the appeal/refund window unless law or a documented dispute requires otherwise. Separate operational records from optional marketing consent.

## Participant rights and routes

The published notice must explain how to:

- obtain a copy of the participant’s submitted information;
- correct inaccurate information;
- withdraw optional marketing or quotation permission;
- request deletion where available;
- report a privacy or security concern;
- complain about access, conduct, billing, or the deliverable;
- learn whether a request cannot be fulfilled and why.

## Incident handling

On suspected unauthorized disclosure, misdelivery, access failure, or data loss:

1. contain the issue;
2. preserve only necessary evidence;
3. notify the designated owner;
4. assess affected people and obligations;
5. correct delivery or access;
6. communicate accurately without speculation;
7. record remediation and prevention;
8. reopen release approval if the incident exposes a systemic defect.

This protocol is not a substitute for a jurisdiction-specific privacy, records, security, or breach-response review.
