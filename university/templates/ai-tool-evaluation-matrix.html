01What this is+

A weighted scoring matrix for choosing between AI tools. You decide which criteria matter and how much each one counts, score every tool on your shortlist against the same scale, and let the weighted math surface a ranked answer. It doesn't replace judgment — it makes your judgment explicit, so a decision you can defend replaces a gut call you can't.

It works on a whiteboard, in a spreadsheet, or in the plain-text version below. What matters is that everyone scores against the same criteria with the same key, before anyone falls for a logo.

02Who it's for+
  • Owners and operators picking between two or three AI tools, tired of feature lists that all read the same.
  • Department leads who have to justify a purchase to finance, IT, or a skeptical boss with something better than "it felt right."
  • Anyone running a procurement or pilot who has to show their work — why this tool, on what basis, over the alternatives.
03What's inside+
  • A weighted criteria table with sensible starting weights across capability, security, accuracy, ease of use, cost, integration, and support.
  • A fully worked example scoring three tools, with the arithmetic shown so you can watch weighting change the outcome.
  • A blank, copy-pastable matrix that drops into a spreadsheet, with a scoring key, the weighted-score formula, and a decision rule.
  • A hard rule on security: a tool that fails a data-security or compliance check is out, no matter how high it scores elsewhere.
04How to use it+
  1. Set criteria and weights first. Before you look at a single tool, agree on what matters and how much. Starting weights are below; adjust them to your business. Weights must sum to 100%.
  2. Define what "good" looks like. For each criterion, write a plain sentence describing a 5. That is what stops scoring from turning into a popularity contest.
  3. Score each tool 1–5. Same scale, same reviewers, same evidence. Score from a real trial where you can, not from the sales deck.
  4. Multiply and total. Weighted score = raw score × weight. Add the weighted scores per tool. Highest total leads.
  5. Apply the hard fail. Cross off any tool that can't meet your data-security or compliance bar, even if it "won" on points. Then pilot the leader before you sign.

Read what the weighting reveals. Tool C scored a perfect 5 on ease of use and cost — the things that feel good in a demo — but its low security score (2) dragged it to last, because security carries real weight. Tool A and Tool B finish neck and neck (4.00 vs 3.95): A leads on raw capability, B leads on security. That is the moment to stop and think, not to trust the second decimal. And note this next part carefully: if your security bar is non-negotiable and Tool C can't clear it, C is disqualified before totals even matter — the 3.30 is irrelevant. Points break ties among tools that pass the gate. They don't let a tool buy its way past a hard fail.

Before you commit: a tool that fails your data-security or compliance requirements is disqualified regardless of its total score — points can't buy a tool past a hard fail. Verify vendor security claims yourself; "we're SOC 2" on a slide is not proof, so ask for the report and read your own contract terms. And run a real, short pilot on your actual work before you sign — a demo shows the tool at its best, a pilot shows it at yours. This is general education, not legal advice — check your own regulatory and contractual obligations before moving any data.

Use it responsibly

What supports this—and where it stops.

Keep the practical material attached to its evidence, method, assumptions, and limits.

Evidence

  • Current vendor documentation and terms
  • Representative task results
  • Security and privacy responses
  • Cost and integration data

Method

  1. Set weights before viewing scores
  2. Distinguish documented claims from observed performance
  3. Test the intended tier
  4. Preserve uncertainty
  5. Require qualified security and legal review where needed

Assumptions

  • The workflow and acceptance criteria are defined
  • Time-sensitive vendor information is independently verified

Limitations

  • Scores depend on chosen weights and evidence quality
  • Products, pricing, policies, and model routing change
