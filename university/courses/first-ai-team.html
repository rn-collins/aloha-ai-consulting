02The contracts under the names are the product+

What you build

Names and personas do not create a team. A usable system has a bounded workflow, distinct responsibilities, least-authority access, inspectable handoffs, evaluation evidence, and a human who can stop the work.

  • Workflow Contract and system map
  • Agent Role Contracts
  • Permission Matrix
  • Human Control record
  • Evidence Ledger
  • Bounded release decision
03A supervised course build is not a production agent system+

01Block 1 — See one workflow as a system+

  1. Choose a bounded, reversible workflow

    45 minutes

    Choose one recurring workflow with a clear trigger, known inputs, a reviewable output, and a human owner. Prefer a low-consequence task that can be rerun or discarded without sending, spending, publishing, changing a system of record, or exposing sensitive data.

    Open lesson →

  2. Map trigger, inputs, steps, decisions, output, and owner

    55 minutes

    Map the work as it exists before assigning agents: trigger, accepted inputs, transformations, decision points, sources of truth, handoffs, output, approval, and accountable owner. Record where judgment, confidential information, or external authority enters.

    Open lesson →

02Block 2 — Decide what AI may and may not do+

  1. Classify steps as off-limits, augment, or automate

    50 minutes

    Classify each workflow step separately. Off-limits means the system may not perform or infer the step. Augment means it may prepare options or a draft while a named person decides. Automate means a deterministic, reversible action may run inside explicit limits.

    Open lesson →

  2. Design the human-control and stop conditions

    50 minutes

    Human review is a control only when the reviewer has time, competence, evidence, authority, and a real ability to reject or change the output. Name the approval object, approver, evidence shown, allowed decisions, and what the system does while approval is absent.

    Open lesson →

03Block 3 — Design the team and its contracts+

  1. Create roles from work, not personas

    60 minutes

    Split the workflow where responsibilities, evidence needs, or review standards differ. A role exists because it owns a bounded transformation—not because a team diagram needs more characters. One role may research, another draft, and another check, but none should approve its own consequential work.

    Open lesson →

  2. Limit tools, data, memory, and handoffs

    60 minutes

    Grant each role only the data, tools, actions, and retention it needs for the current step. Separate read, draft, recommend, write, send, publish, purchase, delete, and administer permissions. A prompt prohibition is not an access control.

    Open lesson →

04Block 4 — Run, evaluate, and decide whether to expand+

  1. Run representative cases and preserve evidence

    70 minutes

    Test normal, ambiguous, incomplete, adversarial, and prohibited cases. Preserve the input class, system and rule versions, outputs, sources, handoffs, approvals, failures, corrections, latency, and cost needed to reproduce the judgment.

    Open lesson →

  2. Package the supervised workflow and make a go/no-go decision

    60 minutes

    Package the workflow map, role contracts, permission matrix, human-control record, evidence ledger, known limitations, incident and rollback procedure, owner, and review date. The package must let another person understand what the team may do and challenge the evidence for expanding it.

    Open lesson →

Assessment and evidence contract

What completion would require.

9 assessments, 5 named sources, one capstone, and a weighted rubric.

Assessments

  • Workflow suitability diagnostic: Complete every field; unresolved consequence, ownership, reversibility, or data questions require choosing a different workflow.
  • Workflow Contract knowledge check: At least 4 of 5 answers correct; retry with lesson feedback.
  • Workflow mapping lab: Every trigger, decision, handoff, source, approval, output, and owner field must be represented or marked unresolved.
  • Authority-boundary knowledge check: At least 4 of 5 answers correct; any approval-bypass answer requires retry.
  • Missing-approval scenario: The response must stop the external action, preserve the record, and route to the named owner.
  • Agent Role Contract lab: Every role has accepted inputs, permitted work, output, prohibited acts, escalation, checks, and a human owner.
  • Permission Matrix lab: No role receives send, spend, publish, delete, administer, or sensitive-data access without separate explicit authorization.
  • Adversarial handoff and tool-use scenario: Identify the untrusted input, prevent unauthorized action, preserve evidence, and specify recovery.
  • First AI Team capstone review: Score at least 80 with no zero in human control, permissions, evidence, or recovery.

Capstone evidence

  • Workflow Contract and map
  • Agent Role Contracts
  • Permission Matrix
  • Human Control record
  • Evidence Ledger with normal, ambiguous, incomplete, adversarial, and prohibited cases
  • Failure, correction, and recovery records
  • Bounded go/no-go decision with owner and review date

Rubric

  • undefined: 15% — Scope, non-goals, trigger, inputs, decisions, output, dependencies, and ownership are complete.
  • undefined: 15% — Roles are minimal, distinct, testable, and human-owned.
  • undefined: 20% — Least authority, data boundaries, retention, and permission changes are documented.
  • undefined: 20% — Consequential actions stop for informed approval with timeout and escalation.
  • undefined: 20% — Representative and failure cases preserve reproducible results, limitations, and corrections.
  • undefined: 10% — Incident, rollback, owner, review date, and bounded go/no-go decision are explicit.

Use it responsibly

What supports this—and where it stops.

Keep the practical material attached to its evidence, method, assumptions, and limits.

Evidence

  • A representative real workflow
  • Example inputs and acceptable outputs
  • Test results and approval records

Method

  1. Start with one useful low-risk workflow
  2. Give every agent a bounded role
  3. Make handoffs explicit
  4. Prevent autonomous sending and spending
  5. Evaluate before scaling

Assumptions

  • A human owner can review every consequential output
  • The first workflow is bounded and reversible

Limitations

  • Named agents do not create accountability on their own
  • External actions require permissions, controls, and human authorization
