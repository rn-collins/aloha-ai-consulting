01A confidently wrong assistant is worse than no assistant+

Why this, why now

Pointing an assistant at stale, contradictory, incomplete, or over-permissioned sources can produce unsupported answers or expose restricted material. The failure can look authoritative because the answer may cite the organization's own documents.

Source governance and system evaluation are separate obligations. This course teaches a bounded pre-retrieval evidence method; it does not guarantee answer accuracy, complete sensitive-data detection, or production safety.

02A checker that grades the source before the assistant reads it+

What you'll build

By the end you have a bounded readiness method and evidence packet for one use case and representative collection: a source register, question map, lifecycle and access controls, evaluation set, remediation backlog, and scoped go, conditional-go, or no-go decision. Production ingestion, security review, access integration, and deployment remain separate work.

Coverage and gaps

Maps expected questions to approved evidence and records unsupported, partial, inaccessible, and out-of-scope cases.

Freshness

Tests review evidence, effective dates, supersession, and change triggers against source-specific rules.

Structure

Inspects whether representative content units preserve meaning, metadata, provenance, and necessary context.

Contradictions

Records duplicate and contradiction candidates for an authorized owner to resolve, exclude, or leave explicitly unresolved.

Sensitive data

Applies the organization's classification and access rules while preserving detector limits, false-negative risk, and required human review.

Human signs off

Critical unknowns, conflicts, access failures, and evaluation failures block a readiness decision until an accountable owner reviews them.

03Nine modules, every one ships part of the real system+

Curriculum

Each module produces part of a reviewable evidence packet. The open course does not ingest organizational documents, connect permissions, or deploy a retrieval system.

  1. Define readiness before retrieval. Bound one use case and write the criterion-level rubric, owners, unknown states, and hard stops.
  2. Inventory sources and owners. Register representative sources and resolve authority, provenance, ownership, access class, and disposition.
  3. Map questions, coverage, and gaps. Connect expected questions to approved evidence, abstention, and classified gaps.
  4. Govern freshness and lifecycle. Test review evidence and design supersession, retirement, and change-trigger behavior.
  5. Prepare structure and metadata. Create addressable units that preserve meaning, context, source location, and provenance.
  6. Expose duplicates and contradictions. Preserve lineage and route unresolved conflicts to accountable owners.
  7. Apply access and sensitive-data controls. Classify content, test least access, and preserve detector uncertainty and escalation.
  8. Evaluate retrieval and answers. Run representative, edge, adversarial, permission, stale-source, conflict, and no-answer cases.
  9. Issue a bounded readiness decision. Prioritize remediation and document what may enter retrieval, under which conditions, and until when.

08Questions+

FAQ

Do I need to know how to code?

No code is required to complete the evidence method and browser-local self-assessment. Implementing ingestion, access enforcement, retrieval, evaluation automation, or production deployment requires the relevant technical and security capabilities.

Do I need a RAG system already?

No. The course evaluates a bounded collection before retrieval. Its conclusions apply only to the sampled sources, stated use case, permissions, evidence, and date; they do not establish organization-wide readiness.

What about sensitive data?

Classification, access, and escalation are core steps, but automated detection is fallible. Do not upload real sensitive documents to the public course tools. Authorized security, privacy, legal, compliance, and information owners remain responsible for the governing rules and release decision.

What is the AI tutor?

A planned course assistant intended to answer from reviewed materials and cite the relevant lesson. It is not currently connected or available; its scope and refusal behavior must be evaluated before release.

Refunds?

01Define readiness before retrieval+

Bound the use case and write the readiness standard.

  1. Name the use case and consequence

    40 minutes

    A knowledge base is ready only for a defined use case, audience, decision, and consequence. A collection may support low-risk discovery while remaining unfit for customer, legal, clinical, personnel, or financial answers.

    Open lesson →

  2. Write the readiness rubric

    45 minutes

    Define observable criteria for authority, coverage, freshness, structure, conflict, access, and evaluation. Do not collapse them into one reassuring score.

    Open lesson →

02Inventory sources and owners+

Create a source register and resolve authority and ownership.

  1. Create the source register

    50 minutes

    Inventory each candidate source by stable identifier, title, system, format, owner, audience, authority, review date, access class, and disposition. A folder listing is not enough.

    Open lesson →

  2. Resolve authority and ownership

    45 minutes

    Authoritative means authorized for the stated question, not merely recent or frequently accessed. Record which source controls when policies, contracts, manuals, FAQs, and working notes disagree.

    Open lesson →

03Map questions, coverage, and gaps+

Define expected questions and expose unsupported answers.

  1. Build the expected-question map

    50 minutes

    Coverage must be tested against questions the system is expected to answer. Gather representative questions across roles, vocabulary, task stages, edge cases, and consequential decisions.

    Open lesson →

  2. Record gaps without fabricating answers

    45 minutes

    A gap exists when no approved source supports the expected answer, the evidence covers only part of the question, or the organization has not made the underlying decision.

    Open lesson →

04Govern freshness and lifecycle+

Record review evidence, expiry rules, and retirement.

  1. Measure freshness with evidence

    45 minutes

    File modification time does not prove substantive review. Preserve effective date, last reviewed date, reviewer, review basis, superseding source, and next review trigger.

    Open lesson →

  2. Design review, supersession, and retirement

    45 minutes

    Every included source needs a review trigger, owner, supersession rule, archive treatment, and deletion or retention basis. Retrieval should not treat current and retired versions as peers.

    Open lesson →

05Prepare structure and metadata+

Make units addressable while preserving context and provenance.

  1. Create addressable content units

    50 minutes

    Prepare content units that preserve headings, definitions, exceptions, tables, citations, and relationships. Chunking that severs a condition from its rule can produce a fluent but wrong answer.

    Open lesson →

  2. Preserve metadata and provenance

    45 minutes

    Every retrievable unit should point back to its authoritative source, version, owner, access class, effective dates, and exact location. Derived summaries require their own provenance and review state.

    Open lesson →

06Expose duplicates and contradictions+

Group duplicates and route unresolved conflicts to owners.

  1. Separate copies from competing sources

    45 minutes

    Exact duplicates, near-duplicates, localized variants, historical versions, and independently authoritative sources are different states. Similarity alone cannot decide which one governs.

    Open lesson →

  2. Route contradictions to human owners

    50 minutes

    Contradictions may involve rules, dates, definitions, thresholds, exceptions, or scope. Automated detection can surface candidates but cannot invent the organization's controlling decision.

    Open lesson →

07Apply access and sensitive-data controls+

Classify content and enforce access before retrieval.

  1. Classify sensitive and restricted content

    50 minutes

    Inventory personal, confidential, privileged, regulated, contractual, security-sensitive, and secret-bearing content under the organization's actual policies and obligations.

    Open lesson →

  2. Enforce access before retrieval

    50 minutes

    Retrieval must respect source authorization, user identity, purpose, tenancy, and current permission. Filtering after generation is too late because restricted content may already have entered model context or logs.

    Open lesson →

08Evaluate retrieval and answers+

Build expected-answer cases and inspect failure modes.

  1. Build the retrieval evaluation set

    55 minutes

    Create representative, edge, adversarial, permission, conflict, stale-source, and no-answer cases before evaluating a system. Predeclare expected evidence, expected answer boundary, and required abstention.

    Open lesson →

  2. Interpret results without a false readiness score

    50 minutes

    Report failures by use case and control: retrieval coverage, evidence precision, citation traceability, answer support, abstention, permission enforcement, freshness, and conflict handling.

    Open lesson →

09Issue a bounded readiness decision+

Prioritize remediation and decide what may enter retrieval.

  1. Prioritize the remediation backlog

    45 minutes

    Translate gaps into bounded work: decide policy, appoint owner, update source, resolve conflict, repair format, change permission, add evaluation, exclude material, or narrow the use case.

    Open lesson →

  2. Issue a scoped go, conditional go, or no-go

    55 minutes

    A readiness decision applies only to the tested collection, use case, permissions, configuration, evaluation set, and date. It is not a certification of the organization or a guarantee of accurate answers.

    Open lesson →

Assessment and evidence contract

What completion would require.

10 assessments, 5 named sources, one capstone, and a weighted rubric.

Assessments

  • Readiness standard review: Use case, consequence, users, owners, criteria, unknown states, and hard-stop rules are explicit.
  • Source register and authority review: Every sampled source has provenance, authority, owner, review state, access class, and disposition or an explicit unresolved state.
  • Question coverage and gap review: Representative questions map to approved evidence, abstention, owner, and a classified gap where support is absent.
  • Freshness and lifecycle review: Review evidence, thresholds, supersession, retirement, missed-review, and change-trigger behavior are documented.
  • Structure and provenance review: Representative units preserve meaning, source location, version, owner, access, and reconstruction evidence.
  • Duplicate and contradiction review: Duplicate lineage and unresolved conflicts remain visible, owner-routed, and blocked from consequential answers.
  • Classification and permission review: Sensitive-data uncertainty, least access, denial, revocation, cache, export, log, and escalation cases are represented.
  • Retrieval and answer evaluation review: Expected outcomes precede tests and every critical unauthorized, unsupported, stale, conflict, or abstention failure remains visible.
  • Remediation and release review: Backlog and decision are bounded by tested use, collection, permissions, configuration, date, owner, evidence, and invalidating changes.
  • Knowledge readiness capstone review: Score at least 85 with no zero in authority, access control, conflict handling, evaluation, or release boundary.

Capstone evidence

  • Use-case contract and criterion-level rubric
  • Source register, authority hierarchy, owner and lifecycle evidence
  • Question map, expected answers, approved sources, gaps and exclusions
  • Structure, provenance, duplicate, conflict, classification and permission records
  • Representative and adversarial retrieval and answer evaluation results
  • Prioritized remediation backlog and bounded go, conditional-go, or no-go decision

Rubric

  • undefined: 15% — Use, consequence, criteria, owners, non-goals, and hard stops are explicit.
  • undefined: 15% — Authority, ownership, freshness, supersession, structure, and provenance are evidenced.
  • undefined: 20% — Questions, gaps, duplicates, contradictions, exclusions, and owner dispositions remain visible.
  • undefined: 20% — Classification, least access, denial, revocation, cache, export, and log controls are tested.
  • undefined: 20% — Representative and adversarial cases have predeclared outcomes and case-level results.
  • undefined: 10% — Release scope, unresolved risk, remediation, rollback, change triggers, and review date are explicit.

Use it responsibly

What supports this—and where it stops.

Keep the practical material attached to its evidence, method, assumptions, and limits.

Evidence

  • Canonical documents and ownership records
  • Version and review dates
  • Representative retrieval questions and expected answers

Method

  1. Audit the source before the assistant
  2. Measure coverage and freshness
  3. Resolve contradictions or expose them
  4. Apply permissions before retrieval
  5. Evaluate answers against known evidence

Assumptions

  • Document owners can identify authoritative versions
  • Sensitive-data and access rules are available

Limitations

  • Source preparation cannot guarantee perfect retrieval
  • Incomplete or conflicting organizational knowledge must remain visible rather than being silently resolved
