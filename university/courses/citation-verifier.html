01A fake citation is no longer a small mistake+

Why this, why now

Courts have sanctioned lawyers for filings containing fabricated and mis-stated authorities. "It sounded right" is not a verification method. For anyone who publishes sourced claims — lawyers, clerks, researchers, journalists, and policy writers — an unverified citation creates professional risk.

The problem is not just whether a source exists. It is whether the source actually supports the specific proposition it is cited for. That is the gap this course teaches you to close, with a tool you build and control.

02A verifier that checks the claim, not just the link+

What you'll build

The public course teaches you to build or specify a bounded system that takes a document and its citations and returns a per-citation review record with evidence. A working deployment depends on the learner's implementation, approved sources, testing, and human-review environment.

Every citation and its claim

Pulls each citation and the exact proposition it is cited for.

Exists and supports

Confirms the source is real and retrievable, then whether it actually supports the claim.

Fabricated, mis-stated, unsupported

A verdict per citation with the supporting passage or the gap.

A shareable audit

Verdicts, evidence, and confidence, ready to hand to a supervisor.

An audit trail

Every check recorded, so it is provable and not merely asserted.

Human signs off

On doubt it defaults to flag for review. You stay accountable; the tool assists.

03Nine modules, each producing inspectable evidence+

Curriculum

You do not watch and forget. You leave each module with a working piece of your verifier.

  1. The problem and the standard. Why fabricated citations happen, the 2026 consequences, and what verified really means. You write your verification rubric.
  2. Set up your workspace. Accounts, model access, project structure, with zero assumptions.
  3. Extract citations and claims. Build the step that pulls each citation and the exact proposition it supports.
  4. Existence and retrieval check. Verify each source is real and reachable; handle dead links and wrong reporters.
  5. The support check, the hard part. Does the source support the specific claim? Build the evidence-grounded core.
  6. Verdicts and report. Supported, unsupported, mis-stated, fabricated, or can't-verify, with evidence and confidence.
  7. Governance layer. Guardrails, uncertainty, default-to-human, and a full audit log.
  8. Evaluate your verifier. Build a test set; measure catch rate and false positives; iterate.
  9. Put it to work and package it. Run it on your real documents, and if you want, how to offer it as a service.

01Define what verified means+

Separate citation form, source existence, current status, quotation accuracy, and proposition support before choosing technology.

  1. The five different citation failures

    35 minutes

    A citation can be malformed, point to no source, identify the wrong source, quote inaccurately, or fail to support the proposition. Those are different questions and require different evidence.

  2. The verification contract

    45 minutes

    Define accepted inputs, covered citation families, approved sources, possible verdicts, evidence returned, abstention rules, and the person authorized to release work.

02Design the bounded workspace+

Choose the data boundary, authority hierarchy, record structure, and human roles that govern the build.

  1. Choose the data path before the model

  2. Create the review-unit schema

03Extract citations and propositions+

Turn a document into review units without pretending that pattern matching understands the legal proposition.

  1. Build a transparent citation parser

    60 minutes

    Start with a narrow citation family and deterministic patterns. Return the matched text and parsed fields so a learner can see exactly what the parser recognized.

    Open lesson →

  2. Bind each citation to the proposition

    55 minutes

    A citation is usually attached to a sentence, clause, quotation, or string of propositions. Capture enough context for review while preserving the exact proposition being tested.

    Open lesson →

04Retrieve and identify the authority+

Resolve a citation against an approved source and preserve the exact result a reviewer inspected.

  1. Rank sources by authority and purpose

    45 minutes

    Use official court or government material when the task requires an authoritative rule or filing. Research databases and aggregators may be useful for discovery but have different coverage, licensing, status, and citator boundaries.

    Open lesson →

  2. Retrieve, normalize, and preserve

    60 minutes

    Normalize the query, preserve the original citation, log the source and retrieval time, and return zero, one, or multiple candidates without forcing a match.

    Open lesson →

05Test proposition support+

Compare the proposition with the retrieved passage and distinguish direct support, qualified support, conflict, silence, and ambiguity.

  1. Locate the supporting passage

    55 minutes

    Retrieve candidate passages around meaningful terms, quotations, headings, and citations. Preserve page or paragraph locators where the source provides them.

    Open lesson →

  2. Compare claim, qualification, and conflict

    65 minutes

    Test who, what, when, jurisdiction, conditions, degree, and exceptions. A source may support a narrower proposition while the draft overstates it.

    Open lesson →

06Issue bounded verdicts+

Make every verdict inspectable, reversible, and no stronger than the evidence.

  1. Use a verdict taxonomy that cannot hide uncertainty

    40 minutes

    Use bounded labels such as structurally invalid, no source located, identity ambiguous, supported, supported with qualification, contradicted, unsupported, and not verified.

    Open lesson →

  2. Produce an inspectable review report

    50 minutes

    A reviewer needs the proposition, original citation, normalized record, source link, passage, verdict, reason, unresolved issue, reviewer status, and version in one place.

    Open lesson →

Place named review, confidentiality, escalation, and release controls around the verifier.

  1. Keep professional responsibility with the professional

08Evaluate failure, not fluency+

Test against representative errors, measure false assurance, and document coverage instead of advertising an unbounded accuracy rate.

  1. Build the failure-focused test set

    60 minutes

    Construct representative cases for valid citations, malformed forms, nonexistent authorities, wrong metadata, inaccurate quotations, overstatement, contradiction, ambiguous mapping, unavailable sources, and unsupported formats.

    Open lesson →

  2. Measure dangerous errors

    55 minutes

    Report results by failure class. False passes are especially consequential because they create unwarranted assurance; false flags create review burden and may also hide systematic gaps.

    Open lesson →

Assessment and evidence contract

What completion would require.

6 assessments, 5 named sources, one capstone, and a weighted rubric.

Assessments

  • Diagnostic: distinguish the verification layers: Diagnostic only; record the baseline and revisit it after Module 6.
  • Knowledge check: contract and verdict boundaries: Earn at least 80 percent and correct every answer that would permit an unsupported pass.
  • Lab: parser and claim-unit coverage: Account for every test citation as parsed, partially parsed, unsupported format, or missed; silent loss is an automatic revision.
  • Lab: retrieval and proposition evidence: Earn at least 80 percent with a source, passage, locator, and bounded reason for every resolved review unit.
  • Scenario: confidential filing under time pressure: Identify all prohibited transfers and require a named authorized reviewer before release.
  • Capstone: build and defend the verifier: Earn at least 80 of 100 overall, earn at least half of every criterion, and receive no automatic-fail finding.

Capstone evidence

  • Verification contract and architecture diagram
  • Source and data-flow register
  • Working bounded prototype or executable technical specification
  • At least twenty independently labeled test cases
  • Per-class evaluation results and error analysis
  • Sample audit record and human-review gate
  • Deployment decision memo
  • Ten-minute defense showing one success and one abstention

Rubric

  • Verification contract: 15% — Inputs, outputs, coverage, verdicts, non-goals, and human authority are testable.
  • Technical implementation: 20% — The system runs or is specified reproducibly, preserves failures, and matches its status label.
  • Evidence traceability: 20% — Every resolved result exposes the authority, passage, locator, and reason.
  • Evaluation: 20% — Independent labels, denominators, per-class results, and error analysis support the release decision.
  • Governance and privacy: 15% — Data boundaries, review authority, escalation, audit, retention, and rollback are operational.
  • Defense and limitations: 10% — The learner demonstrates a success and abstention and answers without overstating capability.

Use it responsibly

What supports this—and where it stops.

Keep the practical material attached to its evidence, method, assumptions, and limits.

Evidence

  • Primary sources and authoritative source records
  • Claim-to-source comparisons
  • Known-good and known-bad citation test cases

Method

  1. Define what the verifier must establish
  2. Separate source existence from claim support
  3. Return inspectable evidence for every finding
  4. Require human review for ambiguous or consequential claims
  5. Evaluate before expanding coverage

Assumptions

  • Learners can supply representative claims and citations
  • Final publication decisions remain with an accountable human

Limitations

  • A verifier cannot guarantee that every source is complete or authoritative
  • Course content is educational and does not replace professional legal or editorial review
