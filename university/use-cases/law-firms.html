01What this use case is+

The idea

AI for law firms means using language models to accelerate the drafting-heavy, document-heavy parts of practice — first-draft memos, contract summaries, deposition digests, discovery review, client intake — while treating every factual and legal assertion as unverified until a lawyer checks it against a real source.

The distinction that matters: general-purpose chatbots make up case citations. Purpose-built legal research tools reduce that risk by grounding answers in real databases, but independent studies show even the leading legal tools still produce meaningful error rates. There is no hallucination-free tool. The workflow, not the tool, is what keeps you out of trouble.

02Who this is for+

Who it helps

Solo and small firms

Without associates to absorb the drafting load, yet still answerable for every word that goes out.

Litigation teams

Facing thousands of pages of discovery and deposition transcripts that a person has to read.

Transactional practices

Summarizing and comparing contracts at volume, where speed matters but a missed clause does not forgive.

Intake and operations staff

Triaging inquiries and drafting routine client communication — support work that must never cross into legal advice.

03The business problem it solves+

The problem

Legal work is expensive because it's slow, and it's slow because so much of it is a lawyer reading and drafting by hand. That's real value for the client on the judgment; it's dead weight on the first draft and the document triage.

AI moves the first draft and the triage off the lawyer's desk, which cuts turnaround and cost on routine matters and frees senior time for the analysis clients actually pay for. The catch — and it's the whole ballgame — is that unverified AI output in a legal context isn't a productivity win, it's a malpractice exposure. This use case exists to capture the speed without the exposure.

04What you need going in+

Inputs

The matter documents

Pleadings, contracts, transcripts, correspondence — in a tool cleared for confidential and privileged material.

A verification source

Access to a real legal database (the citator) so every cited authority can be confirmed and checked for validity.

Your jurisdiction and practice rules

Including your bar's guidance on AI use and disclosure. The rules vary; check yours before you start.

A supervising lawyer

Who reviews and takes professional responsibility for every output. This is not a role a tool can fill.

05Suggested tools+

Tools

Purpose-built legal AI

Tools like Thomson Reuters CoCounsel, Lexis+ with Protégé, and Westlaw's AI research ground answers in real case law and offer inline citation checks against a citator. Independent testing (Stanford's Magesh et al.) still found error rates worth taking seriously — verify regardless.

A citator (KeyCite / Shepard's)

Non-negotiable. Confirms a case is real, still good law, and not overruled. Run every AI-surfaced authority through it.

General AI for non-legal drafting

Claude or ChatGPT (business tier) for client emails, intake summaries, and plain-language explainers — never for case law, and never with privileged data on a consumer tier.

Confidentiality and UPL: client data is privileged — use only tools whose terms protect it and don't train on it. And note the line: AI can help a lawyer work faster, but it cannot practice law. Non-lawyers using it to give legal advice risk the unauthorized practice of law.
06Step by step+

The workflow

  1. Confirm the tool is cleared for privileged data and check your bar's AI guidance before you start.
  2. Load the matter documents into the approved legal tool.
  3. Draft the artifact — memo, summary, digest — and ask the tool to cite its sources for every assertion.
  4. Verify every citation in the citator. If a case can't be confirmed as real and good law, it does not go in the document. Full stop.
  5. Check the reasoning, not just the citations. Read the cited passages to confirm they say what the draft claims.
  6. Lawyer review and revision. A supervising attorney edits, verifies, and signs off.
  7. Log it. Record what was AI-assisted and who verified it, per your firm's policy.
07An example prompt+

The prompt

A drafting prompt that builds verification into the request itself:

Draft a short internal research memo on whether [a non-compete signed in California is enforceable against a remote employee who later moved to Texas]. Requirements: - For every legal proposition, cite the specific statute or case, with the citation. - After each citation, add a line: "VERIFY: [what to confirm in the citator]." - If you are not certain an authority exists or is current, say so explicitly rather than citing it. Do not fabricate or approximate a citation. - Flag any point where the answer depends on facts I have not given you. - End with: "This is a drafting aid, not legal advice, and every citation must be independently verified before use."

A strong output still isn't a filing. The memo might correctly note that California voids non-competes by statute (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 16600) while Texas permits reasonable ones (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 15.50), and flag that the choice-of-law question turns on the contract's own clause and the employee's work history — a gap it cannot fill. The lawyer still pulls each statute, confirms it in the citator, applies it to the real facts, and owns the conclusion.

08What a human still owns+

Guardrails

Verify every citation, every time

No case, statute, or quote enters a document until a human confirms it exists and is good law. Courts have sanctioned lawyers who skipped this.

Confirm the reasoning holds

A real citation attached to a wrong proposition is still a wrong proposition. Read the source, not just the cite.

A licensed attorney supervises

Professional responsibility for the work stays with a bar-admitted lawyer — it cannot be delegated to a tool.

Watch the UPL line

AI assisting a lawyer is fine; a non-lawyer or a tool giving legal advice to the public is not.

This is not legal advice. RN Collins is a JD candidate, not a licensed attorney. Nothing here creates an attorney-client relationship or substitutes for advice from a lawyer licensed in your jurisdiction.

Use it responsibly

What supports this—and where it stops.

Keep the practical material attached to its evidence, method, assumptions, and limits.

Evidence

  • Controlling primary authority
  • Verified record materials
  • Applicable rules and client requirements
  • Attorney review and final work product

Method

  1. Start from professional duties
  2. Protect privilege and confidentiality
  3. Verify authority independently
  4. Preserve supervision
  5. Document material review

Assumptions

  • Licensed attorneys remain responsible
  • The firm has approved data and tool controls

Limitations

  • This resource is education, not legal advice
  • AI cannot determine competence, strategy, or the law without accountable lawyers
