What Aloha AI is, in one paragraph

Aloha AI builds AI systems for regulated and reputation-sensitive fields, where a confident wrong answer is a liability rather than a rounding error. The work joins two things the market usually separates: administrative and regulatory law, read from primary sources, and the science of how people decide what to trust. It is run by RN Collins, a neuroscientist by training and a JD candidate in regulatory law, who scopes and builds every engagement directly. The output is designed to survive inspection by a regulator, a funder, or a court.

The engagements, defined

"Partner with us" is not an offer. These are. Each names the work, the deliverable, and who it serves.

Commissioned regulatory intelligence

A sourced brief or monitor on your question

A dated, primary-source analysis of a specific regulatory question or market, delivered with its verification ledger. You get the record; you and your counsel decide what to do with it.

Program & education underwriting

Sponsor the University or a monitor

Fund free public education or a live regulatory monitor. Attribution is clear, and editorial control stays with the practice so the resource keeps its credibility.

Technology implementation

Build the governed AI system

Design and build an AI workflow or intelligence layer inside your organization, with sourcing, human-approval gates, and an audit trail. Scoped as a fixed build plus optional retainer.

Advisory & fractional leadership

A standing owner for AI decisions

Ongoing advisory or a fractional Chief AI Officer engagement: strategy, governance, and accountability held by a named person on a defined cadence.

Framework & methodology licensing

License the Trust Stack or an instrument

Use a published framework, index, or the verification-ledger method inside your own program, under terms that keep the method intact and correctly attributed.

Content & institutional collaboration

Co-produce with attribution

Joint research, co-authored public resources, or an institutional collaboration where roles, rights, and independence are set in writing at the start.

What each side gives and receives

What you receive

  • A named, credentialed owner who does the work personally.
  • Deliverables you can inspect: sourced, dated, and defensible.
  • Clear attribution and reporting appropriate to the engagement.
  • A method that keeps your program's credibility intact.

What you contribute

  • Funding or fees on a defined structure, agreed before work starts.
  • Access to the question, context, or systems the work requires.
  • A point of contact empowered to make decisions.
  • Agreement on the independence terms below.

Independence, and how conflicts are handled

The value of this work depends on it being trusted, which means a funder or sponsor cannot be allowed to shape a finding. Three commitments hold across every engagement. Funding is disclosed on any resource it supports. A sponsor does not review or alter analytical conclusions before publication. Where a commercial relationship could be read as influencing a result, it is named openly rather than hidden. If an engagement cannot honor these, it is the wrong engagement, and I will say so.

How a first conversation goes

A first call is 20 to 30 minutes and covers four things: the problem you are trying to move, which engagement type fits, what independence and attribution terms apply, and what a scoped proposal would include. You leave the call knowing whether this is a fit and what a written proposal would cost and deliver. No pressure mechanics, no manufactured scarcity.

Aloha AI — one-page brief for internal circulation

Prepared for a colleague, committee, or principal evaluating a partnership, sponsorship, funding, or commission.

What it is
A solo practice building AI systems for regulated and reputation-sensitive fields, joining primary-source regulatory law with the science of trust and adoption. Run by RN Collins (neuroscience MS; JD candidate, regulatory law).
The problem it addresses
In regulated work, a fluent but wrong AI answer creates real exposure. Generic AI cannot cite the governing rule, will not flag its own uncertainty, and leaves no accountable human. That gap sits between legal, compliance, and technical teams and usually goes unowned.
What it produces
Sourced, dated intelligence and governed AI systems whose every figure traces to a primary source, whose consequential outputs require human sign-off, and which carry an inspectable audit trail.
Why it is credible
Every published figure is verifiable; the methods, sources, and limits are documented publicly at /methods; the operator is credentialed in both neuroscience and administrative law; and the practice states plainly what it does not claim, including that it is not legal advice.
Engagement types
Commissioned regulatory intelligence; program or education underwriting; technology implementation; advisory or fractional leadership; framework and methodology licensing; content and institutional collaboration.
Independence
Funding is disclosed on anything it supports; sponsors do not alter analytical conclusions; commercial relationships that could affect a result are named openly.
What a first step involves
A 20 to 30 minute call to confirm fit, engagement type, and independence terms, followed by a written, scoped proposal with cost and deliverables. Contact: collins.ra@northeastern.edu.
