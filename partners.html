Before you propose a partnership

Aloha AI works with institutions, funders, research partners, product teams, sponsors, distributors, and licensees to turn complex expertise into durable infrastructure. The partnership is designed around the actual asset: research, data, a framework, curriculum, product, operating system, or maintained intelligence layer.

This record describes possible partnership structures, responsibilities, and boundaries. It does not create a partnership or engagement.

01Different relationships solve different problems.+

Partnership models

01

Institutional build

Commission

An organization funds a defined system for its own users, operations, research, program, or market.

02

Research and development program

Fund

A funder supports evidence generation, public-interest infrastructure, validation, or a reusable platform.

03

Licensing and implementation

Adapt

An existing framework, curriculum, dataset, tool, or system is adapted for a new institution, audience, or jurisdiction.

04

Product or venture partnership

Co-build

The parties combine domain expertise, distribution, capital, technology, data, or operations to create a larger product.

05

Research and evaluation partnership

Validate

A partner contributes participants, data, expertise, review, implementation context, or independent testing.

06

Education and channel partnership

Distribute

A course, complete program, toolkit, or product reaches learners or buyers through an institution or platform.

07

Public tool or knowledge infrastructure

Sponsor

A sponsor supports access while claims, methods, editorial independence, and conflicts remain explicit.

08

Long-term operating home

Maintain

A partner provides the institutional ownership, staff, systems, and funding needed to keep an asset current.

04The agreement must answer more than who pays.+

Governance and rights

01

Independence and conflicts

Funding does not purchase findings.

Methods remain visible; sources, assumptions, limitations, sponsorship, review, and material changes are disclosed where the work is public.

02

Evidence controls claims

A partner may review for confidentiality, accuracy of its own facts, and agreed scope, but unsupported conclusions are not added.

03

Conflicts are managed

Financial, institutional, professional, and personal relationships are identified, documented, and addressed in the agreement and output.

05What an internal sponsor should be able to circulate.+

Champion-ready internal brief

01

The proposal must state

The problem and affected users; the system being built or licensed; why existing approaches are insufficient; deliverables, duration, cost, dependencies, evidence, and professional-review requirements.

02

The approval path must state

The decision-maker and budget owner; legal, security, privacy, procurement, and subject-matter review; IP, data, publication, and commercialization terms; success gates; the next phase; and the owner after the initial engagement.

Accountability record

Evidence, method, assumptions, and limits.

The public record includes the conditions that limit how it should be read.

Evidence

  • The proposed asset, users, institution, and affected stakeholders
  • Each party’s authority, funding, expertise, data, technology, distribution, and maintenance contributions
  • Applicable IP, data, privacy, security, publication, procurement, accreditation, and professional-review requirements

Method

  1. Define the durable asset and institutional problem
  2. Map contributions, dependencies, decision-makers, and reviewers
  3. Set evidence, independence, conflict, and claim controls
  4. Allocate IP, data, publication, commercialization, and maintenance rights
  5. Define success gates and the operating owner beyond the pilot

Assumptions

  • Each party accurately represents its authority and rights
  • The governing agreement is tailored to the actual asset, jurisdiction, users, and professional responsibilities

Limitations

  • This partnership orientation is not a binding offer or agreement
  • Legal, tax, accounting, privacy, security, accreditation, and other professional terms require qualified review
