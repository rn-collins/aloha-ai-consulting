AI strategy and fractional leadership

Give AI a real operating owner.

Aloha AI helps leadership teams move from scattered tools and experiments to an operating model with priorities, decision rights, governance, implementation ownership, and measurable value. The engagement can begin as a contained audit and continue as fractional leadership when the work needs an accountable senior operator.

The operating problem

AI fails when nobody owns the whole system.

A policy cannot implement itself. A vendor cannot decide your institutional priorities. A strategy deck cannot resolve daily exceptions. The missing layer is an operator who can connect leadership intent, real workflows, data, tools, people, risk, and ongoing evaluation.

Experimentation without priorities

Teams adopt tools opportunistically, but no one has defined which use cases matter, what should stop, or how investments relate.

Governance without operation

Policies exist on paper while staff lack concrete permissions, review paths, escalation rules, and approved workflows.

Implementation without ownership

Pilots stall because the organization has no senior person coordinating decisions, vendors, training, evaluation, and maintenance.

What the work covers

The leadership layer from decision to durable operation.

Direction

Strategy and portfolio

Use-case inventory, prioritization, sequencing, investment logic, operating assumptions, and a roadmap leadership can staff and fund.

Control

Governance and decision rights

Roles, permissions, risk classes, human approvals, escalation, records, vendor review, and change-control expectations.

Execution

Implementation ownership

Pilot design, workflow integration, stakeholder coordination, vendor oversight, issue resolution, training, and adoption.

Learning

Evaluation and improvement

Quality measures, incident review, usage evidence, benefit tracking, maintenance cadence, and executive reporting.

Standard deliverables

Artifacts that make the operating model explicit.

AI operating plan

Objectives, priorities, scope, assumptions, sequencing, owners, dependencies, and success measures.

Use-case and risk register

Candidate workflows, value hypotheses, data sensitivity, consequence class, review requirements, and current status.

Decision-rights matrix

Who may propose, approve, operate, review, suspend, and retire each system.

Implementation roadmap

Milestones, pilots, integrations, training, owners, acceptance gates, and maintenance requirements.

Evaluation framework

Accuracy, evidence quality, completion, adoption, escalation, incident, and business-value measures.

Executive reporting

Decision-ready updates showing what changed, what is blocked, what evidence exists, and what leadership must decide.

How an engagement runs

Evidence before recommendations. Ownership before scale.

Map the real work

Review workflows, stakeholders, data, tools, decisions, constraints, current experiments, and existing controls.

Prioritize the intervention

Compare value, feasibility, consequence, adoption burden, and maintenance requirements before choosing what to build.

Define the operating contract

Specify roles, inputs, outputs, evidence, boundaries, human approvals, escalation, logging, ownership, and evaluation.

Implement and test

Run a bounded pilot, collect evidence, resolve failure modes, train users, and decide whether the system is ready to expand.

Operate and improve

Maintain the system, review incidents and metrics, update sources and rules, and report decisions to leadership.

Boundaries
